# CoESCD Platform — EDMS and Workflow Architecture

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The Electronic Document Management System (EDMS) is the most organizationally complex component of the CoESCD platform. It is not a file repository with approval buttons. It is the digital enforcement layer for the formal administrative procedures that govern how a government institution creates, processes, approves, assigns, tracks, and archives its official correspondence and internal decisions.

Every document in a government institution carries institutional authority. An incoming letter from a partner agency demands a traceable response. An internal order from the Chairman must reach its executors with a verified chain of custody. A resolution on an emergency report must link directly to the tasks it creates and the deadlines it imposes. None of this can be handled by a generic workflow engine bolted onto a file store.

The recommended EDMS architecture is a **position-driven, state-machine-governed, classification-enforcing document control domain** that integrates deeply with the organizational hierarchy, task management, file storage, notification, and audit infrastructure of the wider platform — while maintaining strict domain ownership of all document control logic.

The architecture is organized around four governing principles:

1. **Document identity is permanent.** Once a document is registered, its identity, registration number, and initial metadata are immutable. Revisions create new versions; they do not overwrite history.

2. **Workflow routes to positions, not people.** Every routing, approval, and execution assignment targets an organizational position. The person currently occupying that position acts. When a position is vacant or the occupant is absent, formal delegation and escalation rules resolve the gap.

3. **Every workflow action is a first-class event.** Review, approval, rejection, resolution, reassignment, and deadline extension are not just state changes — they are documented events with authorship, timestamp, reason, and delegation context, stored immutably.

4. **Access is need-to-know, not need-to-deny.** Documents are not broadly visible until explicitly distributed. Access is earned through role, workflow participation, organizational position, or explicit grant. Classification imposes a hard ceiling that no role or participation can override.

## 2. EDMS Mission and Role in the CoESCD Platform

### 2.1 What the EDMS Is

The EDMS is the system of record for all official documentary activity of the Committee. It is the authoritative source of truth for:

- The content and official text of all registered documents
- The sequence of actions taken on every document
- The assignments and deadlines generated from documents
- The organizational positions that reviewed, approved, signed, and received documents
- The archive of completed and superseded documents with their full workflow history

The EDMS is **not** a general content management system. It does not store arbitrary files. It does not manage operational project data. It does not replace task management. It governs the formal documentary processes that create, formalize, and archive the organization's official decisions.

### 2.2 Why This Matters for CoESCD Specifically

CoESCD is a government emergency management institution. Its documentary processes are not administrative overhead — they are operational records. An incoming emergency notification that is not registered, not routed to the correct department, and not assigned to an executor is a missed response. An internal order that is not tracked to completion is an uncontrolled operational risk. The EDMS is the system that prevents these failures by forcing all official documentary activity through a controlled, traceable, deadline-aware process.

Furthermore, the institution is subject to government record-keeping obligations. Decisions, approvals, and assignments must be retrievable years after the fact. The EDMS must be designed with archival permanence as a non-negotiable property, not a feature to be added later.

### 2.3 EDMS in Relation to the Broader Platform

Within the CoESCD platform, the EDMS is one of fifteen domains. It is the domain that most directly reflects the formal organizational processes of the institution. It is the consumer of the organizational hierarchy (routing chains), the producer of task assignments (execution orders), the customer of file storage (document attachments), and the primary source of audit events related to institutional decisions.

The EDMS is central but not supreme. It does not own file bytes. It does not own task execution state. It does not own user identities. It integrates with the domains that own those concerns through defined interfaces. This boundary discipline is what makes the domain maintainable and the overall platform coherent.

## 3. EDMS Domain Boundaries

### 3.1 What the EDMS Owns

**Document records and metadata.** Every document in the system has a document record owned by the EDMS. This record contains the document's type, classification, registration number, sender, recipient list, subject, creation date, status, and the complete version history of its content.

**Document lifecycle state machine.** The EDMS owns and enforces the state machine that governs each document's progression from draft through registration, routing, approval, execution, completion, and archival. No external domain can alter a document's state — state changes happen only through the EDMS's own service layer, triggered by authenticated workflow actions.

**Workflow instance records.** When a document enters a workflow (routing, approval, parallel review), the EDMS creates and owns the workflow instance for that document. The instance tracks the current step, the participants at each step, the actions taken, the timestamps, and the delegation context.

**Route step records.** Each step in an active or historical workflow is an EDMS-owned record: which position was targeted, who acted (the occupant of that position, potentially under delegation), what action was taken, when, and what remarks were provided.

**Resolution records.** When a leadership official issues a resolution on an incoming document (assigning responsibility, setting priorities, authorizing a response), that resolution is an EDMS-owned record linked to the document and to the executor assignments it generates.

**Registration records and numbering.** Official registration data — registration numbers, registration dates, the registrar who performed registration, the register book — are EDMS-owned. Numbering is centrally governed by the EDMS.

**Document correspondence links.** The explicit relationships between documents — a response links to its incoming source, an internal order links to the executive resolution that authorized it, a revision links to the document it replaces — are EDMS-owned.

**Deadline and execution control state.** The deadlines imposed on executors by resolutions and the current execution status reports filed by executors are EDMS-owned. They interact with (but do not duplicate) task management.

**Confidentiality classification of documents.** The EDMS owns the classification level of each document and enforces classification-based access at query time through the authorization domain.

---

### 3.2 What Stays Outside the EDMS

**File bytes and file storage.** The EDMS stores attachment metadata (which files are attached to which document, in which version, with what classification) and references to files stored in the FileManagement domain. The EDMS never stores or streams file bytes. File access permissions for document attachments are managed by the EDMS through the FileManagement domain's permission API.

**Task execution state beyond reporting.** When a document resolution creates executor assignments, the EDMS records the assignment (who was assigned, what they must do, by when) and monitors reporting. But the detailed work done to fulfill that assignment — sub-tasks, intermediate progress, linked communications — lives in the TaskManagement domain. The EDMS receives completion reports from TaskManagement; it does not replicate task state.

**User identity and organizational hierarchy.** The EDMS resolves routing by asking the OrgManagement domain who currently occupies the target position. It validates authority by asking the Authorization domain. It does not cache or replicate org structure. When the org structure changes, the EDMS automatically uses the updated structure for new routing decisions.

**Notification delivery.** The EDMS publishes notification events (document routed to you, approval required, execution deadline approaching, document returned for revision). The Notification domain handles delivery channel selection, user preferences, and delivery confirmation. The EDMS does not know or care whether a notification was sent via in-app alert, email, or SMS.

**Audit event storage.** The EDMS emits structured audit events for every significant action. The Audit domain stores them immutably. The EDMS does not maintain its own audit store — it uses the platform audit infrastructure.

**Search index maintenance.** The EDMS publishes indexing events when documents are created, updated, or reclassified. The Search infrastructure maintains the index. The EDMS does not own search indexes.

**Digital signature cryptographic operations.** Future digital signature support will require a separate Signature Service domain that handles cryptographic operations, certificate management, and signature verification. The EDMS integrates with this service at defined signature request and verification points. The EDMS records signature metadata (who signed, when, what certificate was used, what the signed content hash was) but does not perform cryptographic operations.

**Chat and discussion threads.** Discussion threads linked to a document's execution are hosted by the Communication domain. The EDMS may reference a linked channel for a document, but message records are not EDMS data.

### 3.3 Integration Boundary Summary

| Concern | Owner | EDMS Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| File bytes | FileManagement | References files by ID; manages attachment permissions via FileManagement API |
| Task execution state | TaskManagement | Creates execution assignments; receives completion reports |
| Org structure and positions | OrgManagement | Reads positions for routing; never caches |
| Authorization decisions | Authorization | Calls `can()` before every sensitive operation |
| User profiles | UserManagement | Reads display names and contact info for UI rendering |
| Notification delivery | Notification | Publishes events; does not manage delivery |
| Audit storage | Audit | Emits events; does not store them |
| Search index | Search Infrastructure | Publishes indexing events; queries via search API |
| Signature operations | Signature Service (future) | Sends sign requests; receives signed status and hash |
| Comments/discussion | Communication | References linked channel ID; does not own messages |

## 4. Core Document Concepts

### 4.1 Document

The central entity of the domain. A Document is the formal record of an official communication, decision, or action item within or involving the organization. It is distinct from a file — a file is a byte stream stored in file management. A Document is an organizational record with metadata, a lifecycle, a workflow history, and institutional authority.

A Document exists independently of its attached files. A document can have its text entered directly into the system (rich text body), or it can be represented entirely by its attached file(s), or both. The document record is the authoritative artifact; attached files are supporting materials.

### 4.2 Document Type

A Document Type is a configuration record that defines the behavioral template for a category of documents. Document Types govern:

- Which registration series the document belongs to (and therefore how it is numbered)
- The default classification level
- The default workflow template (who must review it, who must approve it, in what order)
- Whether the document requires a resolution
- Whether the document generates execution assignments
- The default deadline policy (how many days executors have by default)
- Whether digital signing is required
- Which departments are the natural senders or recipients
- Document retention period and archival rules

Document Types are configured by the `org_admin` and `document_admin`. Examples for CoESCD: Incoming Correspondence, Outgoing Letter, Internal Order, Emergency Notification, Executive Resolution, Analytical Report, Regulatory Directive, Meeting Minutes, Act/Protocol.

### 4.3 Document Card (Metadata Record)

The Document Card is the structured metadata face of a document — the formal registration card that exists in government paper-based EDMS systems, digitized. It contains all the mandatory fields that make a document officially registered and traceable:

- Registration number and series
- Registration date and time
- Document date (the date stated on the document itself, which may differ from registration date)
- Document type
- Subject/summary
- Sender (external organization or internal position)
- Recipient(s) (positions, departments, or external organizations)
- Classification level
- Deadline (if applicable)
- Resolution (once issued)
- Execution status
- Reference to related documents (in response to, based on, revises)

The Document Card is the primary search and retrieval record. Full-text search operates on document content; metadata search operates on the Card.

### 4.4 Registration Record

The formal act of registration is its own event record, distinct from the document itself. A Registration Record captures: who registered the document (the registrar's position and user), when, under which registration series, and what number was assigned. Registration is a one-time, irreversible event — a document cannot be un-registered, only cancelled with a preserved cancellation record.

### 4.5 Document Number

An official, unique, human-readable identifier assigned at registration. Document numbers follow a defined format by document type and series: series code / sequential number / year. Example: `ВС-0147/2026` (outgoing correspondence, number 147, year 2026).

The number is assigned by the EDMS registration subsystem at the moment of registration, never before. Draft documents have no official number. Numbers within a series are unique and sequential within their year. Cross-year uniqueness is maintained by the year suffix.

### 4.6 Sender and Recipient Model

Senders and recipients exist in two conceptual categories:

**Internal participants** are resolved to organizational positions. "The Chairman" is a position, not a person — whoever currently occupies the Chairman position is the acting sender or recipient. Internal recipients receive the document through the platform's workflow routing.

**External participants** are organizations or individuals outside the platform — partner agencies, oversight bodies, citizens in rare cases. External senders produce incoming documents. External recipients receive outgoing documents through external channels (physical mail, email, government inter-agency systems). The EDMS records external participant data as structured metadata (organization name, contact reference, external reference number) without linking to internal user accounts.

### 4.7 Attachments

Attachments are references from a document to files stored in the FileManagement domain. Each attachment reference records: the file ID in FileManagement, the file's role in the document (primary document body, annex, supporting material, signature file, previous version), the version of the attachment within this document's history, and the attachment's classification (which may be equal to or higher than the document's own classification — a document may have annexes that are more sensitive than the main body).

### 4.8 Document Version and Revision

A document may go through revisions before final registration (in the draft phase) and potentially after registration if it is returned for correction. Each saved revision creates an immutable version record containing the document's content and attachment snapshot at that point in time, the author of the revision, and the reason for revision.

The current version is the head of the version chain. The EDMS always displays the current version by default. Historical versions are accessible through the version history to users with appropriate access.

Revisions to a registered document that change its official meaning are handled through document supersession, not versioning — the old document is marked superseded and a new document with a new registration number references it.

### 4.9 Workflow Instance

When a document enters a formal workflow process (routing, review, approval), the EDMS creates a Workflow Instance record. The Instance captures: which workflow template was applied, the current step, the ordered list of all steps with their targets and expected actions, the instance's start time and deadline, and its current status.

A document can have at most one active Workflow Instance at a time. A document that is returned for revision causes the Workflow Instance to be suspended pending re-submission. Re-submission creates a new Workflow Instance (referencing the same document) or resumes the suspended one from the appropriate step, depending on the return reason.

### 4.10 Route Step

A Route Step is one node in a Workflow Instance. It records: the target position (resolved at routing time to the current occupant), the step type (review, approval, endorsement, signing, notification), the expected action, the deadline for this step, whether the step is active or complete, the action taken (if complete), the timestamp, the acting user, any delegation context, and any remarks.

### 4.11 Resolution

A Resolution is a formal directive issued by a leadership official on an incoming or internal document. It is the leader's formal response to the document: what must be done, by whom, and by when. The Resolution is a structured record containing: the issuing position, the resolution text (the instruction), one or more executor assignments, the primary executor, the deadline, and the priority level.

Resolutions are the primary mechanism by which documentary content becomes operational work. A resolution on an incoming emergency notification becomes the command that mobilizes a response.

### 4.12 Executor Assignment

An Executor Assignment is a record created by a Resolution or by the EDMS workflow that designates a specific position as responsible for completing a defined action by a defined deadline. It records: the assigned position, the assignment text, the deadline, the priority, the status (assigned, accepted, in-progress, completed, overdue), and the linked Task ID in the TaskManagement domain (created when the assignment is converted to a task).

### 4.13 Execution Status Report

Executors file periodic reports on their progress against executor assignments. An Execution Status Report records: the filing position, the report text, the current reported status, any requests for deadline extension (which require approval), and attachments if any. Status reports are the documentary basis for supervisory monitoring of execution.

### 4.14 Comment and Workflow Remark

Workflow Remarks are the structured notes attached to specific Route Step actions — the reviewer's observations, the approver's conditions, the reason for returning for revision. These are formal workflow records, not chat messages. They are permanently linked to the workflow action that produced them and are part of the document's formal history.

Comments (ad-hoc notes from authorized participants not tied to a specific workflow action) are distinct from Remarks. Whether ad-hoc comments are stored in the EDMS or in the Communication domain's linked channel depends on the deployment configuration — but Remarks are always EDMS-owned.

### 4.15 Workflow History

The complete, immutable, chronological log of every action taken on a document across all of its workflow instances. The Workflow History is the document's institutional memory — a supervisor reading a document can see exactly who did what, when, under what delegation, with what remarks, from draft to archive.

The Workflow History is distinct from the platform-level Audit Log. The Workflow History is business-visible (department heads and supervisors can read it for documents in their scope); the Audit Log is security infrastructure accessible only to authorized auditors.

## 5. Document Lifecycle Model

### 5.1 Universal Lifecycle Principles

All document types share a common lifecycle structure, with variations by type. The lifecycle is a formal state machine — every valid state transition is defined, every invalid transition is rejected with a logged explanation, and every transition produces an event record.

Documents can only move forward through states unless explicitly returned for revision (which moves them backward to a bounded previous state, not to an arbitrary state). Cancellation is a terminal state that can be reached from most active states but never from "Archived."

### 5.2 Shared Document States

```
DRAFT → PENDING_REGISTRATION → REGISTERED → [type-specific flow] → COMPLETED → ARCHIVED
                                                                  ↘ CANCELLED
```

**DRAFT:** The document exists in the system but has no official status. The creator and their supervisors can see and edit it. No workflow has started. No notification has been sent outside the creator's immediate team. A draft has no registration number.

**PENDING_REGISTRATION:** The document has been submitted for registration by its author. It awaits action by a registrar (clerk authorized to register). The document content is frozen for registration purposes — the author cannot modify the content while it awaits registration without withdrawing the submission.

**REGISTERED:** The registrar has assigned an official registration number and date. The document now has formal institutional existence. From this point, the document identity is permanent. The workflow defined by its Document Type begins.

**COMPLETED:** All required workflow actions have been taken, all executor assignments have been marked complete and verified by the controlling supervisor, and the document has been formally closed. No further operational actions are possible on the document, but it remains accessible per its retention policy.

**ARCHIVED:** The document has passed through the retention review process and been moved to the permanent archive. Archived documents are read-only and accessible only under archive access rules.

**CANCELLED:** The document has been officially withdrawn. Cancellation requires authority (the author or a supervisor in their command chain with cancellation capability) and a recorded reason. A cancelled document's metadata and history remain visible to authorized users; its content is still accessible under the original classification rules. Cancellation is not deletion.

---

### 5.3 Incoming Document Lifecycle

An Incoming Document is a formal communication received from an external source (another government agency, a subordinate organization, a partner body) or from an internal source that is initiating a formal communication upward (an internal report submitted for leadership review).

```
RECEIVED → REGISTERED → REVIEW (optional) → RESOLUTION → EXECUTION → CONTROLLED → COMPLETED → ARCHIVED
```

**RECEIVED:** The document enters the system, typically through the secretariat or correspondence office. It may be physically scanned, electronically received, or entered manually. The entry clerk records the sender, the receipt date, and the subject. The document exists but is not yet officially registered.

**REGISTERED:** The clerks or registrars assign the official incoming registration number (different series from outgoing). The document date (the date on the original document), the receipt date, and the registration date are all recorded separately — these can differ and each is significant.

**REVIEW (optional, configurable per type):** Some incoming documents require preliminary review before reaching leadership. The secretariat or a designated review officer reviews the document, adds a covering note, and routes it forward. This step is configured in the Document Type template. It can be sequential (multiple reviewers in order) or skipped.

**RESOLUTION:** The receiving leadership official (the Chairman, a Deputy, or a Department Head depending on the document's addressee and importance) reviews the document and issues a formal Resolution: what must be done, who must do it, and by when. The Resolution is the pivotal moment where the incoming document becomes an operational action item.

**EXECUTION:** The executor(s) named in the Resolution receive their assignments. They acknowledge receipt, perform the required work, and file progress reports. This phase may span days or weeks depending on the deadline. Sub-assignments can be distributed to subordinate positions if the primary executor has that authority.

**CONTROLLED:** The document is in active execution control. The controlling supervisor (typically the department head or the resolution-issuing official) monitors progress, receives status reports, and may extend deadlines (within their authority) or escalate if execution stalls.

**COMPLETED:** The primary executor files a completion report. The controlling supervisor verifies and confirms completion. The document transitions to Completed. The completion record references the work product (an outgoing response document, a task completion record, an action confirmation).

**ARCHIVED:** Per the Document Type's retention policy, the document transitions to archival status after a defined period post-completion.

---

### 5.4 Outgoing Document Lifecycle

An Outgoing Document is a formal communication from the organization to an external recipient. It must carry the official signature of an authorized official and receive an official outgoing registration number before dispatch.

```
DRAFT → PENDING_REGISTRATION → REGISTERED → REVIEW → ENDORSEMENT → SIGNING → DISPATCHED → ARCHIVED
```

**DRAFT:** Prepared by the responsible author (an analyst, a specialist, a department head). Content drafting may involve multiple revisions and internal consultations before submission.

**PENDING_REGISTRATION:** Submitted to the correspondence office for preparation of registration.

**REGISTERED:** Official outgoing registration number assigned. The document is now an official record even before it is signed and dispatched.

**REVIEW:** Some outgoing documents require internal review and coordination across departments before signing. The workflow routes the document to designated reviewers in sequence or in parallel. Reviewers may endorse (accept without modification), return with remarks (request changes before proceeding), or flag for escalation.

**ENDORSEMENT:** A formal endorsement step where co-signers or co-authorizing officials provide their formal concurrence. Endorsements are weaker than signatures — they confirm agreement and support but the signer of the next step carries final authority.

**SIGNING:** The authorized signing official signs the document. In the current phase, signing is an approval action with the signing official's explicit confirmation. In future phases, this step is integrated with the digital signature service, which will produce a cryptographic signature over the document's content hash.

**DISPATCHED:** The signed document has been formally sent to the recipient(s). The dispatch record includes method of dispatch (physical mail, electronic inter-agency system, email for non-sensitive materials), dispatch date, and confirmation reference if available.

**ARCHIVED:** Per retention policy, after a defined period post-dispatch.

---

### 5.5 Internal Document Lifecycle

An Internal Document is an organizational record circulating entirely within the organization — orders, directives, analytical reports, meeting minutes, internal notifications.

```
DRAFT → PENDING_REGISTRATION → REGISTERED → REVIEW/APPROVAL → RESOLUTION (if applicable) → DISTRIBUTION → EXECUTION → COMPLETED → ARCHIVED
```

Internal documents are the most varied type. An internal analytical report has a different workflow than an executive order. Document Type templates capture these variations. The common thread is that all internal documents that carry operational weight (create obligations, change procedures, authorize actions) must go through a registration and approval process before taking effect.

**DISTRIBUTION:** After approval and signing, internal documents are distributed to their intended recipients. Distribution is tracked — the system records which positions received the document and when they acknowledged receipt.

**ACKNOWLEDGEMENT:** Recipients formally acknowledge receipt of documents that require it (orders, directives). Acknowledgement is a workflow step, not just a read receipt. An unacknowledged distribution step can trigger escalation.

## 6. Registration and Numbering Architecture

### 6.1 Registration as a Controlled Event

Registration is the formal act that transforms a submitted document into an official institutional record. It is performed by authorized registrars (clerks with the `document.register` capability scoped to their registration series). Registration is not automatic and is not performed by document authors.

This separation — between authorship and registration — reflects the separation of duties in formal government correspondence processes. The author controls the content. The registrar controls the official record. Neither can perform the other's function.

### 6.2 Registration Series Architecture

The EDMS maintains multiple parallel Registration Series, each governing a distinct category of documents. A Registration Series defines:

- Its series code (a short alphanumeric prefix identifying the document category)
- Its scope (organization-wide, department-specific, or document type-specific)
- Its numbering format (series code / sequential number / year)
- Its sequence counter (maintained atomically to prevent number duplication)
- The role required to register documents in this series
- Whether numbers reset annually or are perpetual
- The register book (the official list of all documents in this series)

**Examples of Registration Series for CoESCD:**
- `ВС` — Incoming Correspondence (all incoming external documents)
- `ИШ` — Outgoing Correspondence (all outgoing letters and formal responses)
- `ФБ` — Internal Orders and Directives
- `АТ` — Analytical Reports
- `ПА` — Emergency Notifications (a separate series for rapid-response incoming)
- `ПТ` — Meeting Protocols

### 6.3 Numbering Rules and Uniqueness

Within a Registration Series, numbers are sequential per calendar year. The sequence counter is managed by the EDMS with strict atomicity — a sequence number is reserved at the moment of registration and is never reused, even if the registration subsequently fails. A gap in the sequence is visible in the register book and is logged, but a gap does not represent a data integrity failure — it represents a reserved number that was not used (possible in edge cases of failed transactions).

Uniqueness is guaranteed by the combination of series code + number + year. The plain registration number (the human-readable form) is also guaranteed unique within its series and year. The EDMS rejects any attempt to create a duplicate.

### 6.4 Incoming vs. Outgoing vs. Internal Numbering Differences

**Incoming** numbers record the document as received. They also preserve the sender's reference number (the number the sender assigned to the document in their own system). The EDMS stores both — the internal incoming number and the external sender's reference — and both are searchable. Cross-referencing by external reference number is essential for responding to correspondence and linking the organization's outgoing response to the incoming document it answers.

**Outgoing** numbers are assigned at registration, before signing and dispatch. This is deliberate — the outgoing number appears on the signed document itself, making it a traceable official record even before physical dispatch. The dispatch record references the registration number.

**Internal** numbers follow the same series-based structure but may be scoped to a specific department series for documents that are purely departmental. Organization-wide internal orders use an organization-level series.

### 6.5 Draft vs. Registered States

Draft documents have no registration number. They are identified internally by the system by a non-human-readable internal ID. When authors refer to a draft before registration, they use the internal ID or a draft reference (e.g., "DRAFT-2026-00892"). Draft references are never official numbers and must never appear on documents submitted externally.

The transition from Draft to Registered is the boundary between an informal working document and an official institutional record. This boundary is enforced by the EDMS state machine — a document cannot receive a registration number while still in Draft state.

### 6.6 Registration Audit

Every registration event is audit-logged with: the registrar's identity and position, the timestamp, the series and number assigned, the document's content hash at the time of registration (to detect post-registration modification of the version content), and the document type. The registration record itself is immutable — it cannot be amended after creation.

## 7. Workflow and Routing Architecture

### 7.1 Workflow Engine Design Principles

The EDMS workflow engine is a **configured state machine interpreter**, not a general-purpose business process engine. It executes defined workflow templates against document instances. This design provides:

- Predictable behavior: every execution of the same template against the same document type produces the same structure
- Auditability: the template applied, the steps generated, and every transition is recorded
- Configurability without code changes: new document types with different workflows are configured, not developed
- Resilience to org changes: routing targets are positions resolved at step-activation time, not at workflow-creation time

The workflow engine does not use a general-purpose BPMN runtime. BPMN engines are powerful but operationally complex and difficult for government administrators to maintain. The EDMS uses a simpler, purpose-built workflow model that covers the specific patterns needed: sequential steps, parallel steps, conditional branching based on document properties, return-for-revision loops, and escalation.

### 7.2 Workflow Template Structure

A Workflow Template is a configured sequence of Step Definitions. Each Step Definition specifies:

- **Step type:** Review, Approval, Endorsement, Signing, Resolution, Distribution, Execution, Acknowledgement
- **Target resolver:** how to find the target position(s) for this step. Resolvers include:
  - `fixed_position(position_id)` — always routes to a specific named position
  - `document_author_supervisor` — routes to the direct supervisor of the document's author
  - `department_head(department_ref)` — routes to the current head of the specified department
  - `recipient_head` — routes to the head of the recipient department(s)
  - `role_in_department(role, department_ref)` — routes to whoever holds a specific role in a department
  - `organization_leadership(level)` — routes to the leadership position at the specified authority level
- **Parallelism:** whether this step is sequential (one participant after another) or parallel (all participants simultaneously, with a completion threshold — e.g., all must complete, or majority, or any one)
- **Completion threshold:** for parallel steps, how many completions constitute step completion (all, any, majority, specific count)
- **Timeout policy:** how long this step may remain active before escalation triggers
- **Escalation target:** if the timeout fires, where does the escalation go
- **Return behavior:** if this step returns the document for revision, which earlier step does execution resume from
- **Conditionality:** whether this step is active for all documents of this type, or only when a condition is met (e.g., "only if document classification ≥ Restricted" or "only if document count exceeds threshold")

### 7.3 Sequential Workflow

The most common routing pattern for formal government correspondence. Documents flow through positions one at a time. Each position takes its designated action (review, endorse, approve, sign) and passes the document to the next position. No step begins until the previous step is complete.

Sequential workflow is used for approval chains that follow the organizational hierarchy — the department head approves before the Deputy Chairman, who approves before the Chairman. This ordering reflects institutional authority and ensures that subordinate approvals are in place before senior approval is requested.

### 7.4 Parallel Review Workflow

Used when multiple departments or officials must review a document independently before a consolidating approval step. All review positions receive the document simultaneously. Each reviewer acts independently. The workflow advances to the next step when the defined completion threshold is met (typically: all reviewers complete their review).

Parallel review is used for inter-departmental coordination documents where several departments have equal standing and no review takes precedence over another. After parallel review, the workflow typically enters a sequential approval phase where a single authority makes the final decision informed by all reviews.

### 7.5 Secretariat Pre-Processing

Incoming documents and outgoing documents pass through a secretariat (correspondence office) preprocessing step before entering the main workflow. This step is handled by clerical staff with the `document.register` and `document.route` capabilities.

The secretariat's preprocessing functions are:
- Verify completeness of the document (all required fields present, all required attachments included)
- Assign or verify the document type
- Register the document (assign the official number)
- Apply the correct workflow template based on document type
- Route the document to the initial workflow step

The secretariat does not review document content for substance — that is the role of the review and approval steps. The secretariat processes the formal shell of the document.

### 7.6 Leadership Resolution Step

The Resolution step is specific to incoming documents (and some internal documents) that require a leadership directive. It is structurally different from an Approval step:

- **Approval** is a binary decision: the approver accepts or rejects the document as submitted
- **Resolution** is a directive: the official reads the document and issues instructions — this transforms the incoming document into action

The Resolution step is active when the document has reached the designated leadership official. The official issues a Resolution record (as defined in Section 4.11). The system then creates Executor Assignments from the resolution text and activates the Execution phase.

### 7.7 Return for Revision

Any step in the workflow can return the document for revision. The return action:
- Specifies the reason (required, stored as a Workflow Remark)
- Specifies the return target: the return may go back to the immediately preceding step, to the document's author, or to a specified earlier step
- The workflow instance is marked "returned" and the returned step is recorded
- The author (or the target of the return) is notified
- The document transitions to a "pending-revision" sub-state
- After revision and re-submission, the workflow resumes from the step that initiated the return, not from the beginning (unless the return specifically required a full restart)

Return for revision must be distinguished from rejection. A return is reversible — the document can be corrected and resubmitted. A rejection is final — the document's current workflow instance is closed as rejected, and further processing requires creating a new document.

### 7.8 Rerouting and Workflow Override

In exceptional cases, an authorized administrator (`document_admin` role) can reroute a document — redirect its current workflow step to a different target position. This is used when the originally targeted position is no longer appropriate (position dissolved, person transferred) and the normal delegation/vacancy escalation rules do not cover the case.

Rerouting is an administrative override, not a normal workflow operation. It requires a recorded justification, produces a prominent audit event, and is visible in the document's workflow history. Rerouting capability is not available to document authors or regular workflow participants.

### 7.9 Escalation Model

Every active workflow step has an associated timeout. When the timeout expires without the step being completed:

1. The system marks the step as "overdue" — the target position receives an escalation notification
2. If the step remains incomplete after a secondary timeout (configurable per step type), the step is escalated to the target's reporting supervisor — the supervisor receives a notification and gains viewing access to the document
3. The escalation is recorded as a Workflow Remark in the document's history
4. If the step still remains incomplete after a tertiary timeout, the escalation reaches the department head or the document's routing authority

Escalation does not automatically redirect the step — the original target is still responsible for acting. Escalation informs supervisory hierarchy about the delay. The supervisor may choose to directly intervene (which requires override authority), apply pressure through normal management channels, or delegate to an acting official.

## 8. Approval, Resolution, and Signing Model

### 8.1 The Conceptual Distinctions

These five workflow action types are frequently confused in generic document systems. In a government EDMS, they represent distinct institutional acts with different authority, different implications, and different future-digital-signature requirements.

---

**Review**

A review is an advisory action. The reviewer reads the document and provides an opinion, observations, or remarks. A review does not approve or reject — it informs. The workflow can continue regardless of the reviewer's remarks (though remarks are recorded and visible to subsequent approvers). A reviewer who has substantive objections returns the document for revision rather than proceeding — but they cannot unilaterally stop the workflow if the document proceeds to the next step without revision.

Review participants are typically specialists, advisors, or departments with relevant expertise who must be consulted but do not have final authority.

---

**Endorsement**

An endorsement is a formal statement of agreement from a co-authorizing party. It is stronger than a review but weaker than approval. An endorsement says: "I, in my capacity, agree that this document should proceed." Multiple endorsements may be required for inter-departmental documents.

Endorsements can be conditional ("I endorse this document on condition that the following be addressed in execution...") in which case the conditions become formal remarks. Endorsement and conditional endorsement are both valid workflow completions — the conditions become part of the document's execution record.

---

**Approval**

An approval is a decision by an authorizing official that the document is correct, appropriate, and authorized to proceed to the next stage (signing, distribution, or execution). An approval carries authority — the approving official takes institutional responsibility for the document's content.

Approval can be granted or denied. A denial returns the document (with mandatory reason) to a specified earlier step. An approval advances the workflow. There is no conditional approval in the strict sense — if conditions need to be met, the document is returned for revision, not approved with conditions.

---

**Signing**

Signing is the final authority act that makes a document official and actionable. In the current implementation, signing is an approval action performed by the authorized signing official with explicit confirmation ("I sign this document"). In future digital signature implementation, signing will additionally produce a cryptographic signature over the document's content hash, creating a legally verifiable record.

The signing step is distinct from approval in that: (a) only specifically authorized signing officials can sign (the `document.sign` capability is granted only to designated officials), (b) once signed, the document's content is immutable — no further revisions are possible to the signed content, (c) the signed version becomes the official record and any subsequent changes require a new document with a new registration number.

---

**Resolution**

A resolution is an executive directive, not an agreement or authorization act. It is the leadership's instruction to the organization about what must be done in response to a document. Resolution is not an approval of the document — it is a command issued from the document. The incoming emergency report is not "approved" — the Chairman issues a resolution on it, directing the appropriate departments to respond.

Resolution is a creation act: it creates executor assignments. It may be issued by multiple officials (each issuing their own partial resolution), and the document can accumulate multiple resolution records if it requires directives from multiple authority levels.

---

### 8.2 Workflow Action State Outcomes

| Action | Outcomes |
|---|---|
| Review | Complete (with remarks) / Return for revision |
| Endorsement | Endorse / Conditional endorse / Return for revision |
| Approval | Approve / Reject (final) / Return for revision |
| Signing | Sign / Return for revision |
| Resolution | Issue resolution (creates executor assignments) / Defer (escalate to higher authority) |

### 8.3 Multiple Approval Levels and Sequential Authority

When a document requires approval from multiple authority levels (e.g., department head → Deputy Chairman → Chairman), the sequential workflow enforces that approvals happen in order. A document cannot reach the Chairman for approval without first passing the Department Head and Deputy Chairman approval steps.

This sequential ordering reflects two institutional realities: (a) subordinate authorities should not burden senior leadership with documents that fail subordinate review, and (b) the senior official's approval is informed by and incorporates the judgement of subordinate approvals.

If a Deputy Chairman rejects a document, the Chairman step never activates — the document returns for revision without consuming senior leadership's time.

### 8.4 Acting and Delegated Approvals

When an approval step targets a position whose occupant has delegated their approval authority (per the delegation model in the RBAC document), the delegate acts in that step. The workflow action record prominently captures: "Approved by [Delegate User] acting under delegation [Delegation ID] on behalf of [Original Position]."

This attribution is permanent in the workflow history and in the audit record. The delegation record is preserved and linked to the approval event. Future queries about who approved a document return accurate and full delegation context.

## 9. Execution Control Architecture

### 9.1 The Execution Control Concept

Execution control is the EDMS function that tracks whether the work ordered by a document's resolution is actually completed, by whom, and by when. It is the enforcement mechanism for accountability — without execution control, a resolution is just words. With execution control, a resolution is a tracked commitment with a deadline and a verifiable completion record.

Execution control operates across two domains: the EDMS owns the assignment record and the completion reporting obligation; the TaskManagement domain owns the operational task that represents the work being done. These are linked but separate.

### 9.2 Executor Assignment Architecture

When a Resolution is issued, the EDMS creates one or more Executor Assignments. The assignment structure supports:

**Primary Executor:** The position principally responsible for the assignment. The primary executor is accountable for the result and for coordinating any co-executors. If there are multiple executors, the primary executor is explicitly designated — it is not an implicit first-in-list designation.

**Co-Executors:** Positions that contribute to the assignment but are not the primary accountable party. Co-executors may have their own sub-deadlines and may file their own partial completion reports.

**Supervisory Position:** The position in the resolution-issuing official's command chain that monitors execution. Typically the department head of the primary executor's department, or the resolution-issuing official themselves for direct assignments.

**Deadline:** The date by which the primary execution must be complete. Deadlines are set by the resolution-issuing official and may be modified only by the same official or a superior in their command chain, with a modification record.

**Priority Level:** Urgent, High, Normal. Affects notification behavior and escalation timelines.

### 9.3 Assignment Acceptance

When an Executor Assignment is created, the primary executor's position receives a notification. The executor must formally acknowledge and accept the assignment within a defined period (configurable by Document Type). Acceptance is a workflow action — it records that the executor understood and accepted the obligation.

Non-acceptance (which would represent failure to respond) triggers an escalation to the executor's supervisor. An executor may not reject an assignment — they may request clarification or a deadline extension, but the assignment remains until the issuing authority modifies or cancels it.

### 9.4 Sub-Assignment and Delegation of Execution

A primary executor may sub-assign portions of the work to their subordinate positions, within their command authority. Sub-assignments are tracked as child records of the parent assignment. The primary executor remains responsible for the parent assignment even after creating sub-assignments — sub-assignment is delegation of work, not delegation of accountability.

Sub-assignments are limited to one level of delegation without explicit `document.execution.sub_assign_deep` capability. This prevents unlimited delegation chains that dilute accountability.

### 9.5 Progress Reporting

Executors are required to file progress reports at defined intervals (configurable per Document Type's deadline policy). A progress report records: current status, work completed to date, expected completion date (if different from the deadline), any blocking factors, and any files as supporting evidence.

Progress reports are visible to the supervisory position and to the resolution-issuing official. They are EDMS records linked to the assignment.

### 9.6 Deadline Extension

An executor who requires more time than the original deadline allows must file a formal Deadline Extension Request. The request records: the current deadline, the requested new deadline, the justification, and any supporting documentation.

Extension requests are approved by the resolution-issuing official or their designated authority. Approval modifies the assignment's deadline and records the modification with full context. Extensions are not unlimited — each extension requires a new justification, and repeated extension requests on the same assignment are surfaced in execution monitoring reports.

### 9.7 Completion and Verification

When the primary executor considers the assignment complete, they file a Completion Report — a formal declaration with the outcome description, the work product references (documents, files, task IDs), and any relevant remarks.

Completion does not automatically close the assignment. The supervisory position reviews the Completion Report and formally verifies completion — confirming that the work product is acceptable and the assignment obligation is met. Only after verification does the assignment status change to "Completed" and the parent document's execution phase advance.

If the supervisory position does not find the completion acceptable, they return the completion report — the assignment status reverts to "In Progress" with the return reason recorded, and the executor must re-file.

### 9.8 Link to Task Management

Every Executor Assignment that represents substantive operational work is linked to a TaskManagement task. The task is created in TaskManagement automatically when the assignment is created in the EDMS, carrying forward the assignment's description, deadline, assignee (position), and priority.

The two records are linked by a bidirectional reference. Task progress visible in TaskManagement informs the EDMS assignment's visible status. Completion in TaskManagement does not automatically complete the EDMS assignment — the formal completion report and verification steps in the EDMS are still required. This separation maintains the EDMS's authority over the formal record while leveraging TaskManagement for operational work tracking.

### 9.9 Overdue Control and Monitoring

The EDMS's execution control subsystem runs periodic checks against all active assignments with deadlines. It generates:

- **Approaching-deadline notifications:** sent to the executor and supervisor a configurable number of days before the deadline
- **Deadline breach notifications:** sent to the executor, supervisor, and resolution-issuing official on the day the deadline passes without a completion report
- **Overdue escalation:** after a configurable period of overdue status, escalation goes to the department head and appears in the organization's executive monitoring dashboard

Overdue statistics are aggregated in the EmergencyAnalytics domain as an operational performance metric — the percentage of document-generated assignments completed on time is a KPI for organizational effectiveness.

## 10. Document Access and Confidentiality Model

### 10.1 Access Dimensions

Access to a document in the EDMS is governed by the intersection of five dimensions. All five must be evaluated before access is granted. A positive result in one dimension does not override a negative result in a higher-priority dimension.

**Dimension 1 — Capability (Role-Based):**
The user must have the capability permission for the action they wish to perform (`document.view`, `document.edit`, `document.approve`, etc.), derived from their current roles as evaluated by the Authorization domain.

**Dimension 2 — Classification (Hard Ceiling):**
The document's classification level must be ≤ the user's effective clearance level. This is an absolute constraint — no other dimension overrides it.

**Dimension 3 — Scope (Organizational Position):**
The user's positional scope must include the document. A document is in scope for a user if:
- They are the document's creator/author
- Their current position is in the document's active workflow chain
- The document belongs to a department within their authority scope
- They are a designated recipient of the document
- The document has been explicitly shared with them or their position

**Dimension 4 — Workflow State (Contextual):**
Some actions are only available based on the document's current workflow state and the user's role in the current step. Even a user with approval capability cannot approve a document that is not currently routed to their position. The document must be actively pending their action in the workflow.

**Dimension 5 — Explicit Grant (Override Mechanism):**
For cross-department access that falls outside the natural scope, an authorized user can create an explicit access grant. Grants are time-bounded, audited, and revocable.

---

### 10.2 Access Rules by Document State

**Draft:**
Visible only to the author and their immediate supervisor. Drafts are pre-institutional — they carry no authority and should not be broadly visible. Department heads cannot browse all drafts in their department, only drafts submitted to them for review.

**Pending Registration:**
Visible to the author, their supervisor, and the designated registrars who handle that document series. Registration is a controlled action — the registrar pool is small and defined.

**Registered / Under Review or Approval:**
Visible to the author, all workflow participants in the current and completed steps, and the author's supervisory chain. The document is now institutional but still in process. Department-level visibility does not automatically extend to all staff — documents under approval are visible based on workflow participation and positional authority, not department membership alone.

**Registered / Under Resolution:**
Visible to the author, the resolution-issuing official, all executor positions designated in any resolution on this document, and the supervisory chain.

**Registered / Completed:**
Visible per the document's normal scope rules: author, workflow participants, the author's department users with `document.view.department`, users with supervisory scope over the author's department. Completed documents are stable records — the access model is less restrictive for completed records than for in-process records, to support organizational learning and reference.

**Confidential Documents (Classification ≥ Level 3):**
Regardless of the above rules, access is restricted to: the author, workflow participants explicitly included in the workflow (not all users at the author's department level), the supervisory chain up to the clearance ceiling, and explicit grant holders. Department-level browsing does not extend to classified documents.

**Archived:**
Access is governed by archive access rules. Active users typically retain access to archived documents in their historical scope. Access to archived documents outside a user's historical scope requires a formal archive access request through the Administration domain.

---

### 10.3 Visibility of Document Existence vs. Content

A key distinction for classified documents: the existence of a document (its registration number, subject summary, sender, and status) may be visible to a broader audience than its content. A department head may know that a Confidential document exists in their department's register without being able to read its content — if their clearance is insufficient, they see the metadata card with content redacted.

This distinction is important operationally: a supervisor needs to know that a classified document is under their department's purview (to manage workflow and accountability) without necessarily needing to read the content. The system should present a "classified content, access restricted" indicator rather than hiding the document's existence entirely from supervisory positions.

## 11. Attachment and File Integration Model

### 11.1 Document Package Concept

A government document is rarely a single file. It is a package: a primary body (the main text of the letter, order, or report), annexes (numbered supporting documents, tables, maps), supporting materials (referenced reference documents, previous correspondence), and signature files (scanned signatures, future digital signature objects).

The EDMS models this as the **Document Package** — a structured collection of attachment references organized by their role within the document. The package structure is:

- **Primary body:** the main document content (either rich text stored in the EDMS, or a reference to the primary attached file)
- **Annexes:** numbered, titled, with their own classification levels. An annex may be classified higher than the primary document — the access model for the primary document does not automatically grant access to its annexes.
- **Supporting materials:** reference documents, background materials. May include references to other documents in the EDMS (linked by document ID) or references to external files.
- **Signature files:** scanned signatures (current) or digital signature files (future). Immutable once attached at the signing step.
- **Correspondence attachments:** files attached to workflow remarks (reviewer's marked-up version, for example) — linked to the workflow step, not to the document package proper.

### 11.2 Attachment Versioning

Attachments change as a document goes through revisions. The EDMS preserves the attachment package state at each document version snapshot. The current version's attachment package is the operational one. Historical version snapshots provide the exact attachment state at each point in the document's lifecycle.

The EDMS does not re-store file bytes for versioning — it stores references to file versions in the FileManagement domain. FileManagement handles file versioning; the EDMS records which file version was current in the document at each snapshot.

### 11.3 Attachment Classification

Each attachment within a document package has its own classification level, which may be equal to or higher than the document's primary classification. The EDMS enforces attachment-level classification independently — a user who can see the primary document content may still be restricted from viewing an annex that is classified above their clearance.

When displaying the document package, the EDMS presents attachment metadata (name, type, size, classification indicator) for all attachments the user can see the existence of, but restricts download and preview to attachments within the user's clearance. A "classified annex, access restricted" indicator is shown for annexes above the user's clearance.

### 11.4 Preview vs. Download

**Preview** (rendering the document in the browser interface) is a lower-privilege action than **Download** (obtaining a local copy of the file). For classified documents and attachments, download capability may be explicitly restricted even when preview is permitted. This is enforced through the FileManagement domain's permission model — the EDMS sets the attachment's permission record at upload time according to the document's access rules.

Preview is served through a secure viewer that renders the file without allowing direct download or copy-paste of classified content. The viewer is a platform capability, not an EDMS responsibility — the EDMS requests preview access through the FileManagement API.

### 11.5 File Integrity

At attachment upload time, the EDMS records the file's SHA-256 checksum (provided by FileManagement upon successful storage). This checksum is stored permanently in the attachment metadata record. Any subsequent access to the file can verify the current checksum against the stored value. A mismatch indicates post-upload file modification — which should be impossible in a correctly operating system but is detectable if it occurs.

For signed documents, the checksum of the document's primary body at the moment of signing is recorded in the signing event record. This is the pre-cursor to full digital signature support — the integrity record exists from day one.

## 12. Audit, History, and Traceability Model

### 12.1 Two-Layer Records Model

The EDMS operates two parallel but distinct record-keeping layers:

**Business History (Workflow History):** The human-readable, organizationally meaningful record of what happened to a document. Visible to document participants, supervisors, and authorized viewers. Presented as a timeline of significant events: "Created by [User A] at [position] on [date]", "Registered as [number] by [User B] at [date]", "Reviewed by [User C] at [position] with remarks: '...' on [date]", "Approved by [User D] under delegation [ref] on [date]", "Resolution issued by [User E] on [date]: '...'", "Assignment accepted by [User F] on [date]", etc.

**Security Audit Log (via Audit Domain):** The machine-readable, security-oriented record of every system-level event. Not directly user-visible. Accessible only to authorized auditors. Includes every action including reads of sensitive documents, access denials, field-level changes, and administrative operations on documents.

These two layers serve different purposes. The Business History answers "what happened to this document from an organizational process perspective?" The Audit Log answers "who touched this record, when, and from where, including attempted unauthorized accesses?"

### 12.2 EDMS Business History Events

The following events are recorded in the Business Workflow History:

- Document created (draft initiated)
- Draft revised (with revision reason)
- Document submitted for registration
- Document registered (number assigned, registrar recorded)
- Workflow instance initiated (template applied, steps generated)
- Workflow step activated (routed to position X)
- Workflow step completed (action taken, by whom, with remarks)
- Document returned for revision (from step, to step, reason)
- Parallel review initiated / all reviews complete
- Endorsement received (conditional or unconditional)
- Approval granted / rejected (with reason if rejected)
- Resolution issued (full resolution text, executor assignments created)
- Executor assignment created / acknowledged / accepted
- Progress report filed
- Deadline extension requested / granted / denied
- Completion report filed
- Completion verified / returned
- Document completed
- Document archived
- Document cancelled (with cancellation authority and reason)
- Explicit access grant created / revoked
- Document shared (explicit cross-department share)

### 12.3 Audit Log Events (via Audit Domain)

In addition to the Business History, the following events are emitted to the Audit Domain:

- Every view of a document at Classification ≥ Level 2 (who viewed, when)
- Every view of a document by a user outside the normal workflow (explicit grant access, supervisory access)
- Every download of an attachment classified ≥ Level 2
- Every failed access attempt (access denied, with the specific denial reason)
- Every administrative operation on a document (rerouting, deadline modification by admin, access grant creation)
- Every delegation usage in a document workflow action

### 12.4 History vs. Technical Logs

The distinction between the Workflow History, the Audit Log, and application-level technical logs must be clearly maintained:

**Workflow History:** Organized by document. Answers business questions. Visible to authorized business users. Immutable from user perspective (not editoriable), but displayed in a human-readable format.

**Audit Log:** Organized by event. Security-oriented. Visible only to auditors. Tamper-resistant (hash-chained, append-only). Contains technical detail (IP addresses, session IDs, API endpoints) alongside business context.

**Technical Logs:** Application logs (errors, performance, debugging information). Stored in the observability infrastructure. Not security records. Rotated on a short retention schedule.

All three layers are maintained separately. An application error appearing in the technical log does not constitute an entry in the Audit Log unless the error represents a security-relevant event. A workflow step completion appears in the Workflow History and produces an Audit Log event, but the underlying HTTP request details are only in the technical logs.

## 13. Search and Retrieval Architecture

### 13.1 Search Dimensions

The EDMS must support several modes of document search, each serving different user needs:

**Registration number lookup:** Direct retrieval by registration number (incoming series, outgoing series, internal series). This is the primary retrieval mode for clerks and correspondents who know the document's reference. Must be exact-match fast.

**External sender reference lookup:** For incoming documents, retrieval by the sender's reference number (the number the external organization assigned to the document). Essential for linking incoming correspondence to its external origin.

**Metadata search:** Filtering and searching across structured document fields: document type, classification, sender (internal position or external organization), recipient department, date range (document date, registration date, receipt date), status, deadline status (overdue, approaching, complete). These are database-level queries against the Document Card records.

**Full-text search:** Searching the subject field, resolution text, workflow remark text, and (with appropriate permissions) the document's body text. Full-text search is powered by the OpenSearch index maintained by the Search Infrastructure.

**Attachment content search:** For documents where attachment content indexing is permitted (based on classification — content of Level 3+ documents is not indexed for full-text search to prevent sensitive content leakage through search), searching within attachment text content. PDF and office document text is extracted during indexing.

**Semantic/contextual search:** Not in scope for the initial implementation, but the search architecture must be designed so that future AI-powered semantic search over document metadata and content can be layered on top of the existing index without rebuilding the foundation.

### 13.2 Permission-Aware Search

All search results are filtered through the Authorization domain before being returned to the requesting user. A search query returns only documents that the requesting user is authorized to see. This filtering happens at the search layer, not in the application — the search index is queried with an authorization-derived filter that restricts results to the user's accessible document set.

The authorization filter is pre-computed for the user's current context (their roles, positions, active delegations, clearance level) and applied as a filter clause in the search query. This means the search never reveals the existence of documents the user cannot see — the count of results, the list of suggestions, and the result set are all filtered.

For performance, authorization filter computation is cached per user session with invalidation on role/position changes.

### 13.3 Saved Searches and Monitoring Views

Users can save frequently used search configurations as personal or shared monitoring views. These are applied to the user's accessible document set and can be pinned as dashboard views. Department heads, for example, may save a view showing all their department's documents with approaching deadlines — this view refreshes live.

The EDMS provides a set of system-defined monitoring views for specific roles: the Clerk's Registration Queue (all documents pending registration in their series), the Head's Approval Queue (all documents pending the user's approval action), the Executor's Assignment Board (all active assignments with their deadlines).

## 14. EDMS Integration Architecture

### 14.1 Integration with Organizational Hierarchy (OrgManagement)

The EDMS reads the organizational hierarchy for routing decisions but never caches the structure in its own data store. Every time a workflow step is activated, the EDMS calls OrgManagement to resolve the current occupant of the target position.

If a position is vacant at step activation time, the EDMS checks the vacancy escalation rule for that position (defined in OrgManagement) and routes to the escalation target instead. This resolution is recorded in the workflow step: "Routed to [Position X]: currently vacant. Escalated to [Position Y] per vacancy rule."

The EDMS subscribes to `org.position.occupancy_changed` and `org.position.vacancy_created` events from OrgManagement. When a position's occupancy changes mid-workflow (an active step is pending when the occupant changes), the EDMS evaluates whether the new occupant should inherit the pending step or whether a re-routing alert should be raised.

### 14.2 Integration with Authorization

The EDMS calls the Authorization domain's `can()` API before every sensitive operation. No EDMS action modifies a document's state without an authorization check. The EDMS does not maintain its own permission logic — all access decisions are delegated.

For performance, the EDMS pre-fetches and caches the user's effective capabilities for the current session (which documents can they view, can they approve, can they register) to avoid per-action authorization round trips for read operations. Capability cache is invalidated on role change events.

### 14.3 Integration with FileManagement

The EDMS uses the FileManagement domain for all file operations. The integration points are:

- **Upload:** EDMS calls FileManagement's upload API. FileManagement stores the file, returns a file ID and checksum. EDMS stores the file ID as an attachment reference.
- **Access permission setup:** When a document's classification or visibility changes, the EDMS updates the file's access permissions in FileManagement via the permission API to match.
- **Preview/download:** The EDMS provides the UI with a pre-signed URL generated by FileManagement (time-limited, user-specific, access-checked) for secure direct access without routing through the EDMS.
- **Virus scan:** FileManagement performs virus scanning on all uploads. The EDMS attachment metadata records the scan result. A document with an unscanned or failed-scan attachment cannot enter registration.

### 14.4 Integration with TaskManagement

The EDMS creates tasks in TaskManagement when executor assignments are established. The creation event carries: the assignment description, the assignee position, the deadline, the priority, a reference back to the EDMS assignment record, and the authority under which the assignment was created (the resolution's issuing position).

The TaskManagement domain publishes `task.completed` events when executors mark tasks complete. The EDMS subscribes to these events for the task IDs it created, using them as a signal that the executor has filed a completion action — though the EDMS still requires its own formal completion report (the task completion is a necessary but not sufficient condition for closing the EDMS assignment).

### 14.5 Integration with Notifications

The EDMS publishes structured notification events to the Notification domain for:

- Document routed to a user's position for action
- Document approval or signing required
- Document returned for revision
- Executor assignment received
- Deadline approaching (configurable lead time)
- Deadline breached
- Resolution issued on a document
- Completion report filed (to supervisory position)
- Document completed
- Escalation triggered

Each event includes: the triggering document's ID and registration number, the event type, the target user(s) (by position), the urgency level, and a brief context text. The Notification domain handles delivery, preferences, and channels.

### 14.6 Integration with the Audit Domain

The EDMS emits audit events for all business-significant actions via the platform event bus. Audit events include the full context of each action: user, position, delegation reference if applicable, document ID, registration number, classification, the action type, the result, and any structured data relevant to the action.

Audit events are emitted asynchronously but with guaranteed delivery — the EDMS uses the outbox pattern to ensure audit events are not lost even if the Audit domain is temporarily unavailable.

### 14.7 Integration with Search Infrastructure

The EDMS publishes document indexing events when:
- A document is registered (making it discoverable)
- A document's metadata changes (subject, status, classification)
- A document is completed or archived
- A document is cancelled

The indexing event carries the document's full metadata card and (for non-classified or appropriately cleared content) the document body text for full-text indexing. The Search Infrastructure maintains the index asynchronously — there may be a brief lag between a document change and its appearance in search results. This eventual consistency is acceptable for search but is explicitly not the case for direct document retrieval by ID.

### 14.8 Integration with the Future Digital Signature Service

The Signature Service integration is designed at boundary level now, even before the service exists. The EDMS signing step produces:

1. A `signature.request` event carrying: the document ID, the content hash of the primary body and annexes at their current versions, the requesting workflow step ID, and the position of the signing official.

2. The Signature Service (when implemented) receives this request, performs the cryptographic signing operation, and returns a `signature.complete` event carrying: the signature value (cryptographic), the certificate reference, the signing timestamp, and the content hash that was signed.

3. The EDMS stores the signature metadata (not the signature value itself — that is stored in FileManagement alongside the signed document) in the signing step record. The document is marked as signed and the content is immutably frozen.

The EDMS signing step in the current implementation performs a business approval action (explicit confirmation by the authorized official) and pre-populates the signature metadata fields with placeholder values. When the Signature Service is implemented, the placeholder values are replaced with real cryptographic data. The workflow model and the document state machine require no changes.

## 15. Future Digital Signature Readiness

### 15.1 Architectural Readiness Principles

The platform is designed so that digital signature integration is a **capability addition**, not an **architectural change**. The document lifecycle, the signing step, and the document immutability model are designed correctly from day one. Digital signature adds cryptographic verification to an existing well-structured process — it does not require that process to be rebuilt.

### 15.2 Signature States

The EDMS document model includes a Signature Status field on the signing step record from day one. Valid states are:

- `not_required` — document type does not require signing
- `required_unsigned` — signing is required, not yet performed
- `signed_business` — signed via business approval action (current implementation)
- `signed_digital` — signed with a cryptographic digital signature (future)
- `signature_verification_failed` — a verification check detected inconsistency (used by audit processes)

The distinction between `signed_business` and `signed_digital` allows the system to operate in the current mode without pretending digital signatures exist, while making it clear which documents have legally stronger signatures when digital signing is implemented.

### 15.3 Content Hash and Immutability Foundation

The content hash of the document body and annexes at the moment of signing is recorded in the signing step regardless of whether digital signatures are active. This hash serves as:

- An integrity check: any modification to the signed document content (which should be impossible after signing) would be detectable by recomputing the hash
- The input to the future digital signature: when the Signature Service is integrated, it signs exactly this hash using the signing official's certificate
- An audit evidence record: the hash proves what content the official agreed to sign

### 15.4 Signature Request Interface

The Signature Service integration boundary is defined as follows:

The EDMS exposes a `SigningContext` object at the signing step: the document ID, the document type, the signing position, the content hash, the classification level, and the workflow step ID. The Signature Service consumes this object and returns a `SignatureResult`: the signature algorithm, the certificate reference, the cryptographic signature value, the signing timestamp according to a trusted time source, and a verification token.

The EDMS stores the `SignatureResult` as the signing step's signature metadata. No other component needs to change.

### 15.5 Signed Document Immutability

When a document's signing step completes (in either business or digital signing mode), the EDMS transitions the document's content to the `FROZEN` internal state. The document body and annexes that were present at signing are immutable. Any subsequent change to the document's content requires:

1. Creating a new version of the document (which creates a new version snapshot)
2. The new version going through a new signing workflow
3. The original signed version remaining in the version history as the official signed record

Documents that require amendment after signing are handled through supersession: the original remains in the archive as a signed record; a new document with a new registration number is created, referencing the original, and goes through its own full workflow.

## 16. Workflow Configurability and Templates

### 16.1 Why Configurability Matters

Government organizations evolve. Departments are created, merged, and dissolved. Approval chains are adjusted by leadership decision. New document types emerge as new regulatory requirements arrive. A hardcoded workflow model would require software changes for every organizational adjustment — an unacceptable operational dependency for a production government system.

The EDMS uses a **configured-not-coded** approach: workflow templates are data configurations managed by authorized administrators, not business logic embedded in application code.

### 16.2 What Is Configurable

**Document Types:** New document types can be created by the `document_admin` without code changes. A document type configuration specifies: its name, registration series, default classification, workflow template, deadline policy, attachment requirements, and archival rules.

**Workflow Templates:** The sequence of steps, the target resolvers for each step, the parallelism configuration, the timeout policies, and the return behavior are all configurable. A new workflow template can be created, tested in a staging environment, and deployed without application code changes.

**Routing Step Targets:** The target position resolver expressions (which use the typed resolver vocabulary defined in Section 7.2) are configurable. When the organization adds a new Deputy Chairman position that must be included in certain approval chains, the affected workflow templates are updated through configuration, not code.

**Deadline Policies:** Default deadlines, escalation timelines, and extension approval rules are configurable per document type. These can be adjusted to reflect regulatory changes or leadership directives about response time standards.

**Confidentiality Defaults:** Each document type has a default classification that is applied if the author does not specify. This default is configurable and can be adjusted as the organization's classification policies evolve.

**Required Reviewers and Approvers:** Department heads can designate that certain positions within their department must be consulted on specific document types — this is a department-level configuration that augments (but does not replace) the organization-level workflow template.

### 16.3 What Is Not Configurable (Hard-Coded Domain Logic)

Some behaviors are not configurable because they represent invariant domain rules whose violation would compromise the system's integrity:

- The state machine's valid transitions (what states can follow what states)
- The requirement that registered documents cannot be content-modified outside a formal revision process
- The requirement that approval steps must be completed by the position they are routed to
- The audit logging obligation for every significant action
- The classification ceiling enforcement
- The delegation re-delegation prohibition

These are not configuration parameters. They are part of the EDMS domain model and are enforced by the service layer. Allowing them to be configured would allow configuration to compromise security or data integrity.

### 16.4 Template Versioning

Workflow templates are versioned. When a template is modified, the old version is preserved. Active workflow instances continue to use the template version that was active when the workflow was initiated — a mid-process template change does not affect documents already in progress. New documents started after the template change use the new template version.

Template versions are referenced by workflow instances and by the workflow history records. Future audits can reconstruct exactly which template governed any historical document's workflow.

### 16.5 Template Testing and Deployment

Template changes go through a test-and-review process before activation. In the administration panel, a `document_admin` can create a draft template, apply it to a simulated document instance to verify the routing and step generation, then activate it for production use. Activation is an audited event. The system supports a "preview mode" for templates where document authors can see what workflow their document will follow before submission.

## 17. Key Risks and Trade-offs

### Risk 1: Organizational hierarchy drift making routing wrong

**Risk:** The organizational structure in the platform lags behind real-world changes. Documents get routed to positions that are vacant or to departments that have been restructured. Workflows stall or go to the wrong people.

**Mitigation:** Organizational structure management is governed by the `org_admin` role with workflow — not a casual edit. The EDMS surfaces routing failures prominently (a workflow step that cannot resolve its target generates an immediate alert to the document administrator). The EDMS does not silently skip or auto-reroute when routing fails — it alerts and awaits administrative resolution.

---

### Risk 2: Workflow template complexity overwhelming administrators

**Risk:** The flexibility of configurable templates, combined with a complex organization, leads to a proliferation of document types with subtly different templates. Administrators lose track of which template applies to which case. Template bugs cause incorrect routing.

**Mitigation:** Template design is a controlled activity — new templates require review and approval through the `document_admin` and `org_admin` workflow before activation. A template audit view shows all active templates, when they were last changed, and how many documents have been processed under each template. Templates with zero usage in the past year are candidates for retirement.

---

### Risk 3: Execution control becoming a paper exercise

**Risk:** Executors file status reports and completion reports that are formulaic and unreviewed. The controlling supervisor approves completions without real verification. The execution control system becomes a bureaucratic ritual rather than a genuine accountability mechanism.

**Mitigation:** This is partly a human governance problem, not a pure system design problem. The system's contribution: completion verification is not automatic, it is an explicit action by a real person. Overdue statistics are visible at the executive level. Repeated pattern of extensions on the same executor is surfaced in the monitoring dashboard. The system cannot force supervisors to verify carefully, but it can make patterns of poor oversight visible.

---

### Risk 4: Classification creep making the system unusable

**Risk:** Documents are classified too conservatively. Most documents end up at Level 3 or higher. The clearance requirements mean junior staff cannot access documents needed for their work. Workarounds develop outside the system.

**Mitigation:** Classification defaults are configurable and should be set realistically, not defensively. A classification audit process surfaces the distribution of classification levels across document types — if an improbably high proportion of documents are classified above Level 2, the defaults for that document type are likely wrong. Classification is also visible in monitoring: an analyst who cannot access most documents in their scope is a signal that something is incorrectly classified or the analyst's clearance is incorrectly set.

---

### Risk 5: The registration bottleneck

**Risk:** All documents must go through a registrar before entering the workflow. If the registrar pool is small and busy, documents pile up in the "pending registration" queue and workflows are delayed.

**Mitigation:** The registration step is fast — a competent registrar spends 2-5 minutes per document verifying completeness and assigning a number. Registration queues are visible in the clerk's monitoring view. High-volume document types can have a larger registrar pool. For extremely time-sensitive document types (Emergency Notifications), the workflow template can be configured for "self-registration" where a senior authorized user can register without the clerk queue — this is an exceptional configuration requiring explicit authorization.

---

### Trade-off: Template-driven vs. fully dynamic workflows

The template-driven model is chosen over fully dynamic workflows (where workflow participants are designated per-document by the author). The trade-off is flexibility vs. control: dynamic workflows are more flexible but allow users to bypass organizational approval requirements, route to unauthorized participants, and create inconsistent processes. The template-driven model enforces organizational governance at the cost of some per-document flexibility. Exceptional routing needs are handled through administrative rerouting (a governed operation) rather than user-directed dynamic routing.

---

### Trade-off: Immutable registered content vs. operational need for corrections

Once registered, a document's content is protected from casual modification. This causes friction when an author discovers a minor error after registration. The resolution is a formal revision process (which creates a version record) rather than a direct edit. For significant content changes, supersession is required. This friction is intentional and correct — government records must be reliable. The trade-off is slightly higher administrative overhead for corrections in exchange for a provably intact record history.

## 18. Final Recommended EDMS Architecture

The CoESCD EDMS is a **position-driven, state-machine-governed, integration-first document control domain** organized around five non-negotiable commitments:

---

**Commitment 1 — Document identity is permanent from registration.**
Once registered, a document's number, registration date, and registration metadata are immutable records. Content changes produce version records. Supersession creates new documents. Nothing in the system can make a registered document disappear. The archive persists permanently. This is the archival foundation of institutional memory.

---

**Commitment 2 — Workflow is organizational, not technical.**
The workflow engine is a servant of the organizational hierarchy, not an independent process engine. Routes resolve to positions. Positions resolve to current occupants. Occupants change without routes changing. When positions are vacant, formal escalation rules apply. The workflow reflects how the organization actually works — not how a generic BPMN engine works.

---

**Commitment 3 — Every workflow action is a permanent record.**
Reviews, approvals, rejections, resolutions, assignments, completions, overrides, and delegation usages are all first-class domain events. They are stored as structured records in the Workflow History and emitted as structured events to the Audit domain. Nothing significant happens to a document that does not produce a record. The document's history is a complete, traceable story of its institutional life.

---

**Commitment 4 — Access is earned through role, position, workflow participation, or explicit grant — never assumed.**
The default state of a document is restrictive. Users gain access through their organizational position, their workflow role, explicit sharing, or their supervisory authority. Classification imposes an absolute ceiling. There are no backdoors, no "see everything" administrative roles that bypass classification, and no ad-hoc access expansions without audit records.

---

**Commitment 5 — The EDMS is integrated, not isolated.**
The EDMS does not attempt to be a complete enterprise suite. It is a domain within the CoESCD platform. It relies on the Authorization domain for access decisions, the OrgManagement domain for routing, the FileManagement domain for files, the TaskManagement domain for execution tracking, the Notification domain for delivery, and the Audit domain for security records. These integrations are well-defined, bounded, and governed by domain boundaries. The EDMS is powerful precisely because it does its one thing — document control — and delegates everything else to the domain that owns it.
