# CoESCD Platform — Task Management Architecture

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The Task Management domain for the CoESCD platform is not a productivity tool. It is a **command execution and accountability control system** that operates at the intersection of organizational authority, documentary obligation, and operational performance. Every task in this system is either a directive from leadership, an obligation generated by a formal document resolution, or a work item created within the bounds of a defined command authority. Ambiguity in responsibility, accountability, or authority is not a design edge case — it is the failure mode this system is specifically built to prevent.

The architecture is organized around three governing realities of government operations:

**First:** Authority flows downward through a command chain. A task may only be assigned by someone with recognized authority over the assignee's position. This is not a courtesy rule — it is an organizational constitutional principle enforced by the system, not by social norms.

**Second:** Accountability is singular and traceable. Every task has one primary responsible position. Co-executors contribute; the responsible position is accountable. The difference between contributing and being accountable must be architecturally unambiguous. When execution stalls, the system knows precisely where responsibility sits and whose supervisor must be alerted.

**Third:** Tasks are not created in a vacuum. Most operationally significant tasks originate from formal documentary processes — a resolution on an incoming emergency notification, an order from the Chairman's office, a directive from a department head following a strategic meeting. The Task Management domain maintains bidirectional links to these originating documents and synchronizes its lifecycle events with the EDMS. A task completed in isolation from its originating document record is an incomplete audit trail.

The recommended architecture is a **position-anchored, authority-validated, lifecycle-governed task domain** that integrates deeply with the EDMS, RBAC, file management, notifications, and audit infrastructure, while maintaining strict ownership of its own data and business logic.

## 2. Task Management Role in the Platform

### 2.1 What Task Management Is

Task Management is the operational execution layer of the CoESCD platform. Where the EDMS manages the formal documentary record of decisions and obligations, Task Management manages the operational work that fulfills those obligations. Where the RBAC model defines who has authority over whom, Task Management enforces that authority at the moment of work assignment. Where the Audit domain records what happened, Task Management's history layer records what was promised, what was done, and what was delayed.

Task Management sits at the center of the platform's accountability model. It is the system that answers the question every supervisor needs answered: "What did I assign, to whom, when is it due, and where does it stand right now?"

### 2.2 What Task Management Is Not

Task Management is not a project management system for long-running complex projects with dependencies, critical paths, and resource allocation. CoESCD's operational reality involves directive-based work: an order is issued, it is assigned, it is executed, it is reported. Complex multi-project coordination is an emergent need that may be addressed in a future phase, but the initial architecture is designed for the directive-execution-accountability pattern.

Task Management is not a replacement for the EDMS's execution control model. The two coexist with a clear boundary: the EDMS owns the formal record of document-generated assignments, their formal completion reports, and their relationship to the registering document. Task Management owns the operational work state: current status, progress updates, sub-task decomposition, and the working history of execution. They are synchronized but separate.

Task Management is not a communication platform. Discussion threads attached to tasks are stored in the Communication domain. Task Management holds references to those threads and can surface context, but it does not own message records.

### 2.3 Position in the Platform Architecture

Within the fifteen-domain CoESCD platform, Task Management occupies a specific integration tier:

- **Downstream from:** EDMS (receives task creation triggers from resolutions), OrgManagement (validates authority at assignment time), Authorization (validates every operation), IAM (validates identity)
- **Upstream to:** Notification (publishes assignment, deadline, and escalation events), Audit (emits lifecycle events), Analytics (feeds performance metrics), FileManagement (manages attachment references)
- **Peer integration with:** Communication (references discussion threads), EDMS (bidirectional lifecycle sync)

## 3. Domain Boundaries

### 3.1 What Task Management Owns

**Task records and metadata.** The canonical record of each task: its type, source, title, description, priority, deadline, current status, and all structured properties. This record persists for the lifetime of the platform regardless of the task's completion state.

**Assignment records.** The formal link between a task and the position(s) responsible for executing it. Assignment records carry: the assigned position, assignment type (primary responsible, co-executor, observer), the assigning authority (position of the person who created the assignment), the assignment date, any acceptance record, and the current assignment status.

**Task lifecycle state machine.** The Task Management domain owns and enforces all valid state transitions. No external domain can directly change a task's status. State changes happen only through the Task Management service layer, triggered by authenticated, authority-validated actions.

**Progress updates and status reports.** Filed by executors to report on their work progress. These are operational records — distinct from the formal completion reports in the EDMS.

**Task hierarchy records.** Parent-child relationships between tasks, creating sub-task decomposition trees. The hierarchy rules (depth limits, propagation logic, authority validation for sub-task creation) are all Task Management domain logic.

**Task comments and internal notes.** Structured notes attached to tasks by authorized participants. These are short-form operational communications within the task context — distinct from the full chat threads in the Communication domain.

**Deadline and escalation state.** The current deadline for each task, any deadline modification history, and the current escalation status (normal, approaching, overdue, escalated).

**Task links and cross-references.** References to the originating EDMS document and resolution (for document-generated tasks), references to related tasks, references to linked communication channels, and references to task-attached files in FileManagement.

### 3.2 What Stays Outside Task Management

**EDMS execution assignment records.** The formal obligation record created by a document resolution lives in the EDMS. The EDMS assignment record is the institutional commitment. The Task Management task is the operational execution tracker linked to that commitment. The EDMS owns the formal record; Task Management owns the execution state.

**Document content and approval workflows.** Task Management never processes or displays document content. It knows a document reference ID and can surface a link, but it does not read or store document data.

**Organizational hierarchy definitions.** Task Management reads the org hierarchy from OrgManagement to validate assignment authority and to compute supervisory visibility. It never stores a copy of the org structure.

**Authorization decisions.** Task Management calls the Authorization domain's `can()` API before every sensitive operation. It does not implement its own permission logic.

**File bytes and file metadata.** Attachment references point to FileManagement. Task Management stores the file reference (ID, name for display) but never file content.

**Full message threads.** Extended discussion about a task's execution is hosted in the Communication domain's channel linked to the task. Task Management stores the channel reference ID.

**Notification delivery.** Task Management publishes notification events. The Notification domain handles delivery channel selection, preferences, and confirmation.

**Audit event storage.** Task Management emits structured events to the Audit domain. It does not maintain its own audit store.

**Analytical aggregations.** Raw task data is accessible to the Analytics domain for aggregation and reporting. Task Management does not maintain analytical views or computed performance metrics — those are derived by the Analytics domain from task events.

### 3.3 Boundary Summary Table

| Concern | Owned By | Task Management Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Formal assignment record | EDMS | Linked by reference; receives creation trigger |
| Task execution state | Task Management | Full ownership |
| Org authority structure | OrgManagement | Read-only consumer; no caching |
| Permission decisions | Authorization | Calls `can()` per operation |
| File bytes | FileManagement | References only; manages attachment permissions |
| Message threads | Communication | References linked channel ID |
| Notification delivery | Notification | Publishes events |
| Audit records | Audit Domain | Emits events |
| Performance analytics | Analytics Domain | Event-based data feed |

## 4. Core Task Model

### 4.1 Task

The Task is the central entity of the domain. It represents a unit of work with a defined responsible party, a defined deadline, and a traceable lifecycle. A Task is not merely a reminder or a checklist item — it is a formal commitment within an organizational command chain. The task record persists permanently, including after completion, because the audit trail and performance history of the organization depend on its permanence.

A Task exists at one of two levels of formality:
- **Directed Task:** Created by a superior position and assigned downward through the command chain. The assignee has no authority to refuse the assignment — they may only report inability to execute (which itself is a governed action with an explicit state).
- **Self-Initiated Task:** Created by a user for themselves or their team to track operational work that is not formally directed by a superior. These carry lower organizational weight but are still tracked, deadline-controlled, and auditable.

### 4.2 Task Type

A Task Type is a configuration record that defines behavioral defaults for a category of tasks. Task Types specify: the default priority, the default deadline policy (how many calendar days from assignment), whether the task requires acceptance before execution begins, whether it requires formal completion verification by the assigning authority, whether it supports co-executors, and whether it can generate sub-tasks.

Task Types are configured by the `task_admin` role. Examples for CoESCD: Emergency Response Task, Regulatory Compliance Task, Analytical Assignment, Correspondence Response Task, Internal Coordination Task, Leadership Directive Task, Training Assignment.

### 4.3 Task Source

Every task carries a Source attribute that records how it came into existence:

**Manual:** Created directly by a user through the task interface, within their authority scope.

**Document-Generated:** Created automatically by the EDMS when a resolution designates executor assignments. The task's source record carries the EDMS document ID, resolution ID, and assignment ID. This source is immutable — it cannot be changed after task creation.

**System-Generated:** Created by platform automation — escalation triggers that create follow-up tasks, deadline monitoring that creates reminder tasks for supervisors, or integration triggers from external systems (future capability). System-generated tasks carry the triggering system component as their source.

The source is architecturally significant: document-generated tasks are subject to EDMS-synchronized lifecycle rules that manual tasks are not. Their completion must be reported not only in Task Management but also feeds back to the EDMS completion control workflow.

### 4.4 Parent Task and Subtask

A task may be decomposed into subtasks by its primary executor or by the assigning authority. The parent-child relationship creates a task hierarchy tree. The parent task record knows its children; each child knows its parent.

Subtask creation is a governed action: only the primary executor of the parent task (or their supervisors in the command chain) may create subtasks. A co-executor cannot independently decompose the parent task into subtasks. Subtask creation requires that the subtask's assignee is within the creator's command authority.

### 4.5 Assignment Record

The Assignment Record is the link between a task and a participant position. It is a first-class entity, not just a field on the task record, because assignment history is critical: tasks may be reassigned, have co-executors added, or have their primary responsible party changed. Each of these events produces a new assignment record or modifies the existing one with a modification log.

An Assignment Record carries: the target position, the assigned user (the position's current occupant at assignment time), the assignment type, the assigning authority position and user, the assignment date, the expected deadline, the acceptance state, and the current status.

### 4.6 Executor and Responsible Person

**Executor** is the operational term: the person doing the work. There may be multiple executors on a task.

**Responsible Person** is the accountability term: the one position designated as primarily accountable for the task's completion. There is exactly one responsible position per task at any given time. This distinction is critical for escalation logic — when a task is overdue, the escalation goes to the responsible person's supervisor, not to every co-executor's supervisor.

The responsible position is the primary executor by default. It can be changed only by the assigning authority or a superior in the command chain, with a modification record.

### 4.7 Co-Executors

Co-executors are positions that contribute to a task's execution but carry no primary accountability. They receive assignment notifications, can file progress updates, can add comments, can attach files, and can report partial completion of their contribution. They cannot independently complete the task — only the responsible executor files the formal completion.

Co-executor assignments are created by the task creator or by the primary responsible executor within their own command authority.

### 4.8 Status

A task's status is an enumerated, lifecycle-governed value (see Section 5). Status is not a free-form field. Valid statuses form a state machine with defined valid transitions. Status changes are event-producing actions, not simple field updates.

### 4.9 Priority

Priority is an ordered enumeration: Critical, High, Normal, Low. Priority affects notification behavior, display ordering, and escalation timelines. Priority is set by the assigning authority at creation and may be elevated (not lowered without authority) during execution if circumstances change.

### 4.10 Deadline

A task's deadline is the date by which the responsible executor must complete the task. Deadlines are set at assignment. Deadline modification is a governed action: it requires either the assigning authority's approval, the supervisor's approval, or a formal extension request process (mirroring the EDMS extension request model for document-generated tasks).

Deadline history — every modification with its authorization record — is permanently stored.

### 4.11 Progress

Progress is the executor's self-reported estimate of completion percentage combined with a current status narrative. Progress is not automatically computed from subtask completion — it is a human-reported field that reflects the executor's assessment. Progress updates are time-stamped records, not overwritten fields, so the history of progress reporting is preserved.

### 4.12 Comments and Internal Notes

Comments are short operational communications attached to a task by participants with task access. They are Task Management-owned records, distinct from the extended discussion threads in the Communication domain. Comments are appropriate for brief status updates, clarification questions, and blocking issue notifications. Extended operational discussion belongs in the linked communication channel.

Comments have a visibility dimension: a comment may be marked "internal" (visible only to the assigning chain and supervisors, not to the executors) to support supervisory discussion about a task without the executor seeing it.

### 4.13 Attachment

An attachment on a task is a reference to a file stored in the FileManagement domain. Attachments may be added by any task participant (subject to their role permissions). Each attachment reference carries: the file ID, the file name for display, the uploader's identity, the upload date, and the attachment's classification level.

### 4.14 Task History

The Task History is the immutable, append-only log of every significant action taken on a task within the Task Management domain: creation, assignment, acceptance, status change, priority change, deadline change, progress update, comment added, attachment added, subtask created, reassignment, escalation, completion, and closure. Each history entry records the actor, the timestamp, the previous state, the new state, and any associated reason or remark.

The Task History is the domain's own business history record — distinct from the platform-level Audit Log which captures security-relevant access events.

## 5. Task Lifecycle

### 5.1 State Machine Design

The task lifecycle is a strict state machine. Every state is defined. Every valid transition is defined. Transitions that are not explicitly defined are rejected. State changes are not field updates — they are governed events that produce history records, trigger notifications, and may trigger integrations with other domains.

### 5.2 States

**DRAFT** (optional, configurable per Task Type)
The task has been created but not yet formally assigned. Available for task types where the creator needs to prepare the task before assignment (e.g., a complex analytical assignment that needs description refinement). Draft tasks are visible only to their creator and their immediate supervisor. A draft task has no deadline enforcement. Not all task types support the Draft state — Leadership Directive Tasks, for example, skip Draft entirely.

**ASSIGNED**
The task has been formally assigned to a responsible position. The assignment is recorded, the notification has been sent, and the deadline clock has started. The task is now a formal obligation of the assigned position. The responsible executor has not yet acknowledged the assignment.

**ACCEPTED** (optional, configurable per Task Type)
The responsible executor has formally acknowledged and accepted the assignment. This state is used for high-importance task types (Emergency Response, Leadership Directive) where explicit acknowledgement is required to confirm that the assignment has been received and understood. For routine task types, this state may be skipped and the task transitions directly from ASSIGNED to IN_PROGRESS when the executor begins work.

Non-acceptance within a configured time window (the executor fails to acknowledge) triggers an escalation — not an automatic status change. The task remains in ASSIGNED state; the supervisor receives an alert.

**IN_PROGRESS**
The executor has begun work and has filed at least one progress update, or the task has been in ACCEPTED state for longer than the configured "auto-start" period. The responsible position is actively working. Progress updates are filed periodically.

**ON_HOLD**
The executor has flagged the task as blocked — they cannot proceed due to circumstances outside their control (waiting for another task to complete, waiting for a resource, waiting for leadership direction). The ON_HOLD state requires a recorded reason. The hold is visible to the supervisor and the assigning authority. A held task does not advance toward completion but continues to accrue toward overdue if the deadline passes.

The ON_HOLD state is not a mechanism for avoiding work — it is a formal signal that the executor has a specific, documented blocker. Supervisors review hold reasons and either resolve the blocker, extend the deadline, or reassign.

**COMPLETED**
The responsible executor has filed a completion report — a formal declaration that the assigned work is done. The completion report includes: a description of what was accomplished, references to the work product (files, documents, other tasks), and any outstanding observations.

For task types that require supervisor verification, COMPLETED is a transient state pending verification. For routine task types, COMPLETED may be the terminal operational state.

**VERIFIED** (for task types requiring verification)
The assigning authority or the designated supervisor has reviewed the completion report and confirmed that the work product is acceptable and the obligation is met. VERIFIED is the final operational state.

**RETURNED**
The assigning authority or supervisor has reviewed a completion report and found it insufficient. The task is returned to IN_PROGRESS with a recorded reason. The responsible executor must re-execute and file a new completion report.

**REJECTED / CANNOT_EXECUTE**
The executor has formally declared that they cannot execute the task. This is not a refusal — it is a formal report of inability with a specific, documented reason (missing authorization, conflicting priority task, resource unavailability, etc.). CANNOT_EXECUTE requires a supervisor review. The supervisor either resolves the blocker (returning the task to IN_PROGRESS), reassigns the task to a different position, or escalates to the original assigning authority for a decision.

**OVERDUE**
A derived status applied when a task's deadline passes without a COMPLETED or CANNOT_EXECUTE status. OVERDUE is not a state the executor transitions to voluntarily — it is applied by the platform's deadline monitoring process. An overdue task remains in its previous operational state (ASSIGNED, ACCEPTED, IN_PROGRESS, ON_HOLD) with the OVERDUE flag applied in addition. Resolving OVERDUE requires either completing the task, obtaining a deadline extension, or having the task cancelled by the assigning authority.

**CANCELLED**
The assigning authority has formally cancelled the task. Cancellation is a terminal state. The cancellation record includes: who cancelled, when, and why. A cancelled task's history is preserved. Cancellation of a document-generated task produces a synchronization event to the EDMS.

**CLOSED**
Administrative closure of a completed or cancelled task after the defined retention review period. Closed tasks are archived. Their history remains queryable but they no longer appear in operational views.

### 5.3 Valid Transitions

```
DRAFT → ASSIGNED
DRAFT → CANCELLED

ASSIGNED → ACCEPTED (if task type requires acceptance)
ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS (if task type skips acceptance)
ASSIGNED → OVERDUE (by deadline monitor)
ASSIGNED → CANNOT_EXECUTE
ASSIGNED → CANCELLED

ACCEPTED → IN_PROGRESS
ACCEPTED → ON_HOLD
ACCEPTED → OVERDUE (by deadline monitor)
ACCEPTED → CANNOT_EXECUTE
ACCEPTED → CANCELLED

IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED
IN_PROGRESS → ON_HOLD
IN_PROGRESS → OVERDUE (by deadline monitor)
IN_PROGRESS → CANNOT_EXECUTE
IN_PROGRESS → CANCELLED

ON_HOLD → IN_PROGRESS (hold resolved)
ON_HOLD → OVERDUE (by deadline monitor)
ON_HOLD → CANCELLED

COMPLETED → VERIFIED (if verification required)
COMPLETED → RETURNED (if verification fails)
COMPLETED → CLOSED (if no verification required, after retention period)

VERIFIED → CLOSED (after retention period)

RETURNED → IN_PROGRESS

OVERDUE → [remains in prior operational state + OVERDUE flag]
OVERDUE resolves when: task completes, deadline is extended, or task is cancelled

CANCELLED → CLOSED (after retention period)
CANNOT_EXECUTE → [awaits supervisor decision: reassign, cancel, resolve blocker]
```

### 5.4 State Transition Authority

Not every user can trigger every state transition. Authority for transitions:

| Transition | Who Can Trigger |
|---|---|
| DRAFT → ASSIGNED | Creator (within their command authority) |
| ASSIGNED → ACCEPTED | Responsible executor only |
| ASSIGNED / IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED | Responsible executor only |
| COMPLETED → VERIFIED | Assigning authority or designated supervisor |
| COMPLETED → RETURNED | Assigning authority or designated supervisor |
| → CANCELLED | Assigning authority or superior in command chain |
| → ON_HOLD | Responsible executor or supervisor |
| IN_PROGRESS ← ON_HOLD | Responsible executor or supervisor |
| OVERDUE flag | System (deadline monitor) only |
| → CANNOT_EXECUTE | Responsible executor only |
| CANNOT_EXECUTE resolution | Supervisor or assigning authority |

## 6. Assignment and Responsibility Model

### 6.1 Who Can Assign Tasks

Task assignment follows the command chain model established in the RBAC and Organization Model. The assignment authority rules are:

**Direct assignment downward:** A user occupying Position A may assign tasks to any position in A's transitive subordinate set — positions that report (directly or through intermediate levels) to Position A in the reporting relationship graph.

**Self-assignment:** A user may assign tasks to themselves (for tracking their own work).

**Lateral assignment with explicit grant:** Assignment outside the command chain requires a cross-department assignment grant — a formal, audited, time-bounded permission record. Without this grant, attempts to assign tasks outside the command chain are rejected by the Authorization domain.

**System assignment:** The EDMS and platform automation may create tasks programmatically. System-created tasks carry the authority reference of the document or process that initiated them.

### 6.2 Chain-of-Command Enforcement at Assignment Time

When a task is created with a target assignee, the system performs a command chain validation before confirming the assignment:

1. Resolve the creator's current positions and their transitive subordinate sets
2. Check whether the target assignee's current position falls within any of those subordinate sets
3. If yes: assignment is authorized; proceed
4. If no: check for an active cross-department assignment grant covering this creator-assignee pair
5. If no grant exists: reject the assignment with a specific reason and log the failed attempt

This validation is not advisory — the system will not create the assignment record if the authority check fails. The assigning user receives a clear error: "You do not have assignment authority over [Position Name]. Your authority extends to: [list of positions in scope]."

### 6.3 Assignment by Position vs. by Person

Task assignments target **positions**, not individual users. The assignment record specifies the target position. The current occupant of that position is the effective executor. If the position's occupancy changes after assignment (personnel transfer, acting assignment, delegation), the task's effective executor changes accordingly.

This is the correct model for a government institution: the obligation belongs to the position. The system resolves the current occupant at notification time and at each access query, always reflecting the current occupancy state.

Exception: tasks that are inherently personal (a specific individual is named due to their unique skill, not their position) are also supported. In this case, the assignment still formally targets a position but carries a "person-specific" flag, and the system alerts if the position's occupant changes and the named individual is no longer the occupant.

### 6.4 Multiple Executors

Tasks with multiple executors carry exactly one primary responsible position and zero or more co-executor positions. Rules:

- The primary responsible position is designated at assignment time, not defaulted by order
- Co-executors are added at assignment time or during execution (by the primary executor within their own command authority, or by the assigning authority)
- Co-executors can be from different departments only if a cross-department assignment grant covers the relationship
- The primary responsible executor's completion report is required for task completion — co-executor completion reports are tracked separately and are advisory, not determinative

### 6.5 Reassignment Rules

**Initiated by the assigning authority:** The assigning authority may reassign the primary responsible position at any time. Reassignment produces an assignment modification record with the reason. The previous assignee's work is preserved in the task history. The new assignee receives a notification with context.

**Initiated by the responsible executor:** The responsible executor may request reassignment (via a formal request that routes to the assigning authority for approval) but may not unilaterally reassign. Self-reassignment would break the accountability model.

**Triggered by vacancy/transfer:** When a position's occupant changes (vacancy, transfer, acting assignment), the system generates an alert to the new occupant and to the assigning authority. The task does not automatically reassign — human decision confirms whether the incoming occupant inherits the task or it is formally reassigned.

### 6.6 Delegation Support

When a user is operating under a delegation (acting as a position or with delegated task assignment authority), task assignments made under the delegation carry the delegation reference in the assignment record. The audit record and task history show: "Assigned by [User A] acting under delegation [delegation ID] on behalf of [Position B]."

A delegate operating under a task assignment delegation may assign tasks within the scope and command chain of the delegating position — not their own natural command chain. If the delegation scope is narrower than the full position authority, the system enforces the narrower scope.

## 7. Execution Tracking Model

### 7.1 Progress Updates

The responsible executor files progress updates at defined intervals. A progress update contains:

- Current reported completion percentage (0-100, self-assessed)
- Current status narrative (brief operational description of where the work stands)
- Any blockers or dependencies the executor is managing
- Estimated completion date (if different from the deadline — a yellow flag that triggers supervisor visibility)
- Optional file attachments (evidence of work in progress)

Progress updates are time-stamped, immutable records. Subsequent updates do not overwrite previous ones — the full history of progress reporting is preserved and queryable.

### 7.2 Supervisor Oversight

Supervisors in the task's command chain have visibility into tasks assigned to positions within their authority scope. Their oversight interface shows:

- All tasks assigned to their subordinate positions, grouped by status
- Tasks with approaching deadlines (configurable lead time)
- Tasks that are overdue
- Tasks in ON_HOLD or CANNOT_EXECUTE state
- Tasks with no recent progress update (stale tasks — configurable staleness threshold)

Supervisors can take the following actions on subordinate tasks:
- View full task detail including history and progress updates
- Add internal comments (visible to the assigning chain but not to the executor)
- Approve or deny deadline extension requests
- Trigger an escalation (manually escalate to higher supervision)
- Reassign the task (with authority and audit record)
- Cancel the task (with authority and reason)
- Grant a CANNOT_EXECUTE resolution (reassign, resolve blocker, or cancel)

Supervisors cannot modify a task's description, attachments, or priority without the authority level of the original assigning user or higher.

### 7.3 Overdue Detection

The platform runs a scheduled overdue detection process against all active tasks. The process:

1. Scans all tasks with status not in (COMPLETED, VERIFIED, CANCELLED, CLOSED)
2. Compares each task's deadline against the current date/time
3. Tasks past their deadline receive the OVERDUE flag
4. An overdue event is published for each newly overdue task (tasks that were already flagged overdue do not produce repeat events)
5. Notification events are published for the responsible executor, the co-executors, the immediate supervisor, and the assigning authority

Overdue detection runs at configurable intervals (minimum every 15 minutes for Critical priority tasks, hourly for others).

### 7.4 Escalation Logic

Escalation is the automatic notification of supervisory hierarchy when execution fails to meet expectations. Escalation tiers:

**Tier 1 — Approaching Deadline (Warning):**
Triggered when a task's deadline is within the configured warning period (default: 3 days for Normal priority, 1 day for High, 4 hours for Critical). Notifies: responsible executor, direct supervisor.

**Tier 2 — Overdue (First Alert):**
Triggered when the deadline passes without completion. Notifies: responsible executor, direct supervisor, assigning authority.

**Tier 3 — Extended Overdue (Second Alert):**
Triggered when the task has been overdue for a configurable period (default: 3 days for Normal, 1 day for High, 4 hours for Critical). Notifies: responsible executor's supervisor's supervisor, assigning authority's supervisor.

**Tier 4 — Chronic Overdue (Escalation to Department Head):**
Triggered when the task has been overdue for an extended period (configurable per task type, typically 7 days). Notifies: the department head of the responsible executor's department and, for document-generated tasks, the resolution-issuing official.

Each escalation tier produces an audit event. Escalation does not change the task's assigned ownership — it informs supervisory hierarchy. Taking action on an escalated task (reassigning it, extending the deadline, resolving a blocker) is a human decision informed by the escalation alert.

### 7.5 Completion Verification

For task types that require supervisor verification:

1. Executor files a completion report (task enters COMPLETED state)
2. Assigning authority or designated supervisor receives a notification
3. Supervisor reviews the completion report and work product
4. Supervisor either: Verifies (task enters VERIFIED) or Returns (task enters RETURNED, with reason, back to IN_PROGRESS)

The verification requirement is configured at the Task Type level. Leadership Directive Tasks and Emergency Response Tasks always require verification. Routine administrative tasks may not.

For document-generated tasks, task verification in Task Management triggers a synchronization event to the EDMS: the executor assignment's completion status is updated. The EDMS's own formal completion verification (the supervisory verification step in the EDMS execution control model) is a separate action performed in the EDMS interface — the Task Management verification does not replace it, but it informs it.

## 8. Task Hierarchy Model

### 8.1 Parent-Child Structure

A task may be decomposed into subtasks by the primary responsible executor. The decomposition creates a parent-child tree where:

- The parent task maintains its original assignment and deadline
- Each subtask is an independent task record linked to its parent
- Subtasks have their own assignees, deadlines (must be ≤ parent deadline), and statuses
- Subtask assignees must be within the creator's command chain authority

### 8.2 Decomposition Authority

Only the primary responsible executor of the parent task may create subtasks from it — and only for positions within their own command chain authority. This reflects the operational reality: a department head assigned a task may distribute sub-components to their team members. A co-executor may not independently fragment the work into subtasks.

If the primary executor needs to create subtasks for positions outside their command chain (a cross-department coordination need), this requires an explicit cross-department assignment grant, as with any lateral assignment.

### 8.3 Depth Limit

Task hierarchy depth is limited to three levels by default (parent → child → grandchild). Deeper hierarchies become operationally opaque — supervisors lose visibility into what is happening at the execution level, and accountability becomes diffuse. The depth limit is configurable at the system level but requires explicit justification to increase.

### 8.4 Status Propagation

Status propagation in the task hierarchy is directional and conservative:

**Upward propagation (child → parent):**
- A parent task cannot be COMPLETED if it has any active (non-COMPLETED, non-VERIFIED, non-CANCELLED) subtasks. The system enforces this — the completion action on a parent task is blocked until all subtasks are in terminal states.
- A parent task is automatically flagged with a "subtask overdue" indicator when any of its subtasks become overdue, even if the parent's own deadline has not passed. This surfaces the decomposed work's health to the parent task's supervisor.

**Downward propagation (parent → child):**
- Cancelling a parent task triggers a notification to all active subtask assignees that the parent has been cancelled. Subtask cancellation itself requires explicit action — it is not automatic, because the work may need to be preserved as evidence or reassigned.
- Deadline extension on a parent task does not automatically extend subtask deadlines. Subtask deadlines are managed independently by the responsible executor.

### 8.5 Optional Dependencies

Task dependencies (task B cannot start until task A completes) are supported as an optional feature configured at the Task Type level, not enabled by default. Dependencies are useful for structured operational sequences (stage 1 must complete before stage 2 begins) but add scheduling complexity that most operational task usage does not need.

When dependencies are enabled for a task type:
- A task with an unfulfilled dependency enters a BLOCKED sub-state (informational, not a full lifecycle state)
- Completion of the dependency task triggers a notification to the blocked task's executor and an automatic transition out of BLOCKED
- Circular dependency detection is enforced at dependency creation time — the system rejects configurations that would create a cycle

## 9. Integration with EDMS

### 9.1 Task Creation from Document Resolutions

When a Resolution is issued in the EDMS on an incoming or internal document, the EDMS creates Executor Assignments in its own domain (owned by the EDMS) and publishes a `resolution.assignment_created` event. Task Management subscribes to this event and creates a corresponding Task record.

The Task record created from a resolution carries:
- Source type: `DOCUMENT_GENERATED`
- Source reference: EDMS document ID + resolution ID + assignment ID
- The assignment description from the resolution text
- The assigned position (from the resolution's designated executor)
- The deadline (from the resolution's deadline)
- The priority (from the resolution's priority level)
- The assigning authority position (the resolution-issuing official's position)

This task creation is deterministic — the same resolution always produces the same task structure. If the task creation fails (e.g., the assigned position does not exist), the failure is surfaced to the EDMS as a creation error event, and the EDMS flags the assignment as unprocessed.

### 9.2 Bidirectional Lifecycle Synchronization

The link between a document-generated task and its originating EDMS assignment is bidirectional. Key synchronization events:

**EDMS → Task Management:**
- `assignment.deadline_modified` → Task deadline is updated with a modification record referencing the EDMS change
- `assignment.cancelled` (EDMS cancels the resolution or its executor assignment) → Task Management cancels the corresponding task
- `assignment.reassigned` (EDMS changes the executor) → Task Management creates a reassignment record on the task

**Task Management → EDMS:**
- `task.completed` → EDMS executor assignment is notified of completion (the EDMS still requires its own formal completion report, but the task completion is surfaced)
- `task.overdue` → EDMS executor assignment overdue flag is set; the EDMS execution monitoring view reflects the overdue status
- `task.cancelled` → EDMS is notified that the operational task has been cancelled (for investigation — task cancellation without EDMS authorization is an anomaly)
- `task.cannot_execute` → EDMS executor assignment is flagged with the executor's inability report

### 9.3 Audit Linkage

All audit events for a document-generated task carry the EDMS document reference, the resolution ID, and the assignment ID as structured context fields. This means an audit query can traverse from a task's audit events back to the originating document, or from a document's audit events forward to the tasks generated by its resolutions. The audit trail is cross-domain and traceable.

### 9.4 Avoiding Duplication

The document-generated task is the operational tracker. The EDMS assignment is the formal institutional record. The two are never merged — they remain separate records with a stable link. This means:
- Completion in Task Management does not automatically complete the EDMS assignment (prevents gaming the formal record through the operational tool)
- Cancellation in EDMS does automatically cancel the Task Management task (the formal record drives the operational record)
- Viewing a document in the EDMS surfaces a link to the corresponding Task Management task (for context navigation) but does not expose Task Management's internal operational state directly in the EDMS interface

## 10. Access Control Model

### 10.1 Access Dimensions

Access to a task is governed by five dimensions, evaluated by the Authorization domain in sequence:

**Dimension 1 — Capability:**
The user must hold the required capability permission (`task.view`, `task.assign`, `task.complete`, `task.manage`, etc.). Derived from their current roles.

**Dimension 2 — Participation:**
Regardless of role scope, direct task participants — the assigning authority, the responsible executor, co-executors, and explicitly designated observers — always have view access to the task. Participation overrides the lack of positional scope.

**Dimension 3 — Positional Scope:**
Users with supervisory scope over the responsible executor's position can view tasks assigned to positions within their authority scope. A department head sees all tasks assigned to positions within their department and subordinate units. A Deputy Chairman sees all tasks within their command chain.

**Dimension 4 — Classification:**
Tasks may carry a classification level (particularly document-generated tasks inheriting the originating document's classification). A task's classification level is ≤ the originating document's classification. Users whose clearance is below the task's classification can see the task exists but not its content.

**Dimension 5 — Explicit Grant:**
For cross-department observer access (a department head who needs visibility into a task being handled by a different department for coordination purposes), an explicit access grant can be created by the assigning authority. Explicit grants are time-bounded and audited.

### 10.2 Action-Level Access

View, comment, progress-update, complete, verify, reassign, cancel, and deadline-extend are separate capabilities. Not every user with view access can perform all of these actions:

- **View:** Participants + positional scope users
- **Add comment:** Participants + supervisors in the command chain
- **Add internal note:** Supervisors only
- **File progress update:** Responsible executor and co-executors only
- **Complete:** Responsible executor only
- **Verify/Return:** Assigning authority and designated supervisors
- **Reassign:** Assigning authority and superior command chain
- **Cancel:** Assigning authority and superior command chain
- **Extend deadline:** Assigning authority and superior command chain (or designated by delegation)
- **Create subtasks:** Responsible executor (within their own command chain authority)

### 10.3 Confidential Tasks

Document-generated tasks from classified documents carry the originating document's classification. Access to the task's description and content requires clearance at or above the classification level. The task's existence (registration number equivalent, assigned position, deadline) may be visible to supervisors without content access — the same "existence-vs-content" distinction applied in the EDMS.

## 11. Collaboration and Comments Model

### 11.1 Two-Layer Collaboration Model

Task-related collaboration happens at two levels:

**Level 1 — Task Comments (Task Management-owned):**
Short-form structured notes attached directly to the task record. Comments are appropriate for: brief status clarifications, questions about the assignment scope, blocking issue notifications, and supervisor acknowledgements of progress reports. Comments are visible in the task detail view alongside the task history. They are part of the task's permanent record.

**Level 2 — Discussion Threads (Communication domain-owned):**
For tasks that require extended operational discussion — multiple stakeholders, complex coordination, shared decision-making — a linked communication channel is created in the Communication domain. The task record carries the channel reference ID. The Task Management UI surfaces a link to the channel. Message content lives in the Communication domain, not in Task Management.

### 11.2 Comment Visibility Rules

Comments have two visibility tiers:
- **Participant-visible:** Visible to the assigning authority, all executors (primary and co-), and any user with supervisory scope over the task
- **Supervisor-only (internal notes):** Visible only to users with supervisory scope over the task (not visible to the executing positions). Used for supervisory discussion about a task ("This executor has been chronically delayed — escalate to department head if not complete by Friday") without creating an adversarial dynamic with the executor

Internal notes are clearly distinguished in the UI from regular comments to prevent accidental mislabeling.

### 11.3 Mention and Reference Model

Comments support structured references:
- **@position mention:** Notifies a specific position's current occupant. The mention is resolved to the occupant at notification time. If the position changes occupancy, the notification history shows the position reference, not a stale user name.
- **#task reference:** Links to another task in the same comment thread (for related-task cross-referencing)
- **^document reference:** Links to an EDMS document number (for commentary that references formal policy or correspondence)

---

## 12. Attachment Model

### 12.1 Attachment Integration with FileManagement

All task attachments are stored in the FileManagement domain. Task Management stores attachment references (file ID, display name, classification level, uploader identity, upload date) as part of the task's attachment collection. FileManagement handles storage, versioning, virus scanning, access control, and preview/download serving.

### 12.2 Who Can Attach

Any task participant may add attachments to a task, subject to:
- The file's classification level must be compatible with the task's classification context (a file classified above the task's level cannot be attached to the task without elevating the task's classification — which requires authority)
- The uploader must have `file.upload` capability
- Attachment of new files to a VERIFIED or CLOSED task requires administrative authority

### 12.3 Attachment Classification Enforcement

When a file is attached to a task, the EDMS integration pattern is mirrored: the file's access permissions in FileManagement are updated to match the task's access context. Users with task view access gain file view access for attached files. Users whose classification clearance is insufficient for the file's classification level see the file exists (name and type) but cannot preview or download it.

### 12.4 Versioning

If the same file is updated and re-attached (a revised work product, for example), the previous version is not replaced — a new file version is created in FileManagement and a new attachment reference is added to the task. Previous attachment versions remain accessible through the attachment history, which is part of the task history record.

---

## 13. Audit and History Model

### 13.1 Task History (Business Layer)

The Task History is the task's internal, business-readable event log. It is maintained by the Task Management domain, stored in the task domain's own data store, and visible to authorized task participants and supervisors. The Task History answers: "What happened to this task, in what sequence, and who was responsible for each action?"

Every significant action produces a Task History entry:
- Task created (source, creator, initial assignment)
- Task assigned (to whom, by whom, deadline set)
- Task accepted (by executor)
- Status transitions (every state change with actor and reason)
- Priority changed (old and new priority, actor, reason)
- Deadline modified (old and new deadline, actor, authorization reference)
- Progress update filed (reporter, content summary, attachment references)
- Comment added (author, visibility level, content)
- Attachment added (filename, uploader, classification)
- Subtask created (creator, assignee, deadline)
- Reassignment (previous assignee, new assignee, actor, reason)
- Escalation triggered (tier, notified parties)
- Completion report filed (executor, content summary, work product references)
- Verification action (verifier, outcome, reason if returned)
- Deadline extension requested / approved / denied
- Task cancelled (actor, reason)
- EDMS synchronization events (sourced from EDMS integration)
- Delegation usage (delegate identity, delegation reference)

### 13.2 Audit Log Events (Security Layer)

In addition to the Task History, Task Management emits audit events to the Audit domain for security-oriented logging:

- Every view of a task with classification ≥ Level 2 (who viewed, when, from where)
- Every failed access attempt with the denial reason
- Every assignment action (created, modified, cancelled)
- Every deadline extension (including all authorization references)
- Every reassignment
- Every cancellation
- Every delegation usage
- Every administrative override

### 13.3 Retention

Task History records and Audit events for task actions are retained according to the platform's configured retention policy. For government institutional records, the minimum retention period is aligned with the corresponding document retention rules — tasks generated from documents with a 10-year retention schedule are retained for at least 10 years.

## 14. Notification Model

### 14.1 Notification Events Published by Task Management

Task Management publishes the following notification events to the Notification domain. Each event specifies the recipient positions, the event type, the urgency level, and context data:

**Assignment notifications:**
- `task.assigned` → Responsible executor position, co-executor positions (if any)
- `task.reassigned` → New responsible executor position, previous executor position (informational)
- `task.coexecutor_added` → Newly added co-executor position

**Lifecycle notifications:**
- `task.accepted` → Assigning authority position (confirmation)
- `task.on_hold` → Assigning authority position, direct supervisor of executor
- `task.cannot_execute` → Assigning authority position, direct supervisor of executor
- `task.completed` → Assigning authority position, supervisor (if verification required)
- `task.verified` → Responsible executor position
- `task.returned` → Responsible executor position (with reason)
- `task.cancelled` → Responsible executor position, co-executor positions

**Deadline and escalation notifications:**
- `task.deadline_approaching` (Tier 1) → Responsible executor, direct supervisor
- `task.overdue` (Tier 2) → Responsible executor, direct supervisor, assigning authority
- `task.overdue_extended` (Tier 3) → Extended supervision chain (see Section 7.4)
- `task.overdue_chronic` (Tier 4) → Department head level and above

**Change notifications:**
- `task.deadline_extended` → Responsible executor, co-executors
- `task.priority_elevated` → Responsible executor, co-executors, supervisors

### 14.2 Notification Batching and Throttling

For supervisors with large teams, individual task event notifications can create notification overload. The Notification domain applies configurable batching for supervisory users: instead of individual notifications for each of thirty subordinate tasks approaching a deadline, a supervisor receives a daily digest: "14 tasks assigned to your subordinates are due within 3 days."

Critical priority tasks and overdue notifications bypass batching — they are delivered immediately regardless of digest preferences.

### 14.3 Notification Channels

Task Management does not manage delivery channels — it publishes events with urgency levels and recipient positions. The Notification domain maps urgency to channel:

- Critical/Overdue: In-app alert (immediate) + notification in the communication channel linked to the task (if one exists)
- Normal deadline warnings: In-app notification, potentially email digest
- Informational lifecycle events: In-app notification

## 15. Reporting and Analytics

### 15.1 Data Flow to Analytics

Task Management is a producer of analytical data, not an analytics processor. It feeds the Analytics domain through two mechanisms:

**Event stream:** Task lifecycle events published to the platform event bus are consumed by the Analytics domain's ingestion pipeline. The Analytics domain maintains its own aggregated views derived from these events.

**Query API:** The Analytics domain may issue structured queries to the Task Management domain for historical task data, subject to the same authorization constraints as direct task access (the analytics query is scoped to the requesting user's authority scope and clearance level).

### 15.2 Performance Metrics Supported

The following metrics are computable from task data for the Analytics domain:

**Organizational performance:**
- Task completion rate by department (completed on time / total completed)
- Average time-to-completion by task type and priority
- Overdue rate by department, position, and task type
- Average deadline extension count per task type
- Escalation frequency by department
- CANNOT_EXECUTE frequency by position and task type (indicates systemic capacity or authority issues)

**Individual performance:**
- Tasks assigned to a position per period
- Tasks completed on time by a position
- Average task duration by position
- Overdue rate by position
- Extension request rate by position

**Document-generated task metrics:**
- Resolution-to-task-creation latency (time between resolution issued and task acknowledged)
- Document response time (time from document received to resolution task completed)
- Compliance with resolution deadlines by department

**Executive-level visibility:**
- All tasks assigned by leadership-level positions: status distribution, overdue count, completion trend
- Cross-department task count and completion rate (indicates inter-departmental coordination effectiveness)

### 15.3 Scope Enforcement in Analytics

All analytical queries are scoped to the requesting user's authority. A department head's analytics view covers only their department and subordinate units. A Deputy Chairman's view covers their command chain. Platform-wide analytics require organization-wide analytical viewer authority, which is a specifically granted capability, not a default for leadership roles.

## 16. Cross-Department Task Handling

### 16.1 When Cross-Department Tasks Are Allowed

Cross-department task assignment — assigning a task to a position in a department outside the assigning user's command chain — is permitted only under explicit, governed conditions:

**Condition 1 — Leadership authority:** Officials in the Leadership Domain (Chairman, Deputy Chairmen) have organization-wide assignment authority and may assign tasks to any position.

**Condition 2 — Cross-department assignment grant:** An explicitly authorized, time-bounded, scoped permission granted by a `task_admin` or `org_admin`. This grant specifies: the granting authority, the grantee position, the target department or positions, the scope (what types of tasks), and the validity period.

**Condition 3 — Document-generated tasks:** The EDMS resolution system, operating under the authority of the resolution-issuing official, may create tasks for any position within the issuing official's authority — which for senior officials may span departments. These tasks do not require a separate cross-department grant because the resolution's authority derives from the issuing official's position authority.

### 16.2 Visibility Rules for Cross-Department Tasks

When a task is assigned across departments:
- The assigning authority and their command chain see the task in their normal task oversight view
- The assignee's own department supervisor sees the task in their subordinate task view (they need to know what work obligations their positions carry)
- The task appears in both the assigning department's and the receiving department's workload views

This dual visibility may occasionally surface tasks to supervisors who were not involved in their creation. This is intentional: a department head must know what tasks have been assigned to their positions from external authorities — it affects workload planning and may surface conflicting priorities.

### 16.3 Responsibility Boundaries

In cross-department tasks:
- The primary responsible position remains in the receiving department
- The assigning authority remains in the assigning department (or Leadership Domain)
- The responsible executor's department head has operational oversight but cannot cancel or modify the task — only the assigning authority or superior in their command chain can do so
- If there is a conflict between the cross-department task's demands and the department's internal priorities, the responsible executor's department head may request a review from the assigning authority — this is a governance process, not a system function

## 17. Configurability

### 17.1 Task Types

The `task_admin` role can create and manage Task Types without code changes. A Task Type configuration specifies all behavioral defaults and requirements:

- Name and description
- Default priority
- Deadline policy (default days from assignment, by priority level)
- Whether Draft state is supported
- Whether acceptance is required
- Whether completion verification is required
- Whether subtask decomposition is allowed
- Whether dependencies are supported
- Maximum hierarchy depth
- Default classification level
- Notification behavior (which events trigger which notification types)
- Whether the task type supports co-executors
- Whether cross-department assignment is allowed for this type
- Retention period

### 17.2 Priority Schemes

Priority levels are configurable (the four-level scheme is the default). Organizations may configure fewer levels for simplicity or additional levels for operational granularity. Priority-to-escalation-timeline mappings are configurable.

### 17.3 Deadline and SLA Policies

Deadline policies are configured at the Task Type level. A deadline policy specifies:
- Default deadline in working days from assignment
- Extension request approval authority (first-level approver position, second-level approver position)
- Maximum number of deadline extensions allowed before escalation to a higher authority
- Warning period thresholds (when Tier 1, 2, 3, and 4 notifications trigger)
- Whether calendar days or working days are used

Working day calculation supports configurable national holiday calendars — relevant for a Tajikistan government platform where holidays follow the national calendar.

### 17.4 Workflow Rules

Some task types have additional workflow rules beyond the standard lifecycle:
- Required completion report minimum length (prevents one-word completions)
- Required work product attachments (a task cannot be completed without at least one attached file — used for analytical assignments that must produce a report)
- Mandatory escalation recipients for specific overdue thresholds
- Auto-cancellation after chronic overdue (configurable per task type — some task types allow auto-cancellation after a defined period of overdue with no activity)

## 18. Risks and Trade-offs

### Risk 1: Command chain staleness producing invalid assignments

**Risk:** The organizational hierarchy in OrgManagement does not reflect real-world reporting changes. A manager assigns a task to a position that is technically outside their command chain per the platform's records but is de facto their subordinate in the real organization. The assignment is rejected as unauthorized.

**Mitigation:** This is primarily a governance failure — organizational changes must be promptly recorded. The system's rejection of out-of-chain assignments is correct behavior; the solution is updating the org records, not loosening the assignment authority check. The `org_admin` dashboard should surface pending org change requests and flag uncommonly long assignment rejection rates in specific departments as a signal that the recorded hierarchy may be outdated.

---

### Risk 2: The CANNOT_EXECUTE state becoming an avoidance mechanism

**Risk:** Executors learn that filing CANNOT_EXECUTE removes the immediate pressure of an overdue task. The state is used strategically rather than genuinely.

**Mitigation:** CANNOT_EXECUTE does not close the task — it routes to supervisor review, which adds supervisory attention rather than removing it. CANNOT_EXECUTE frequency by position is a tracked analytical metric. Repeated CANNOT_EXECUTE filings from the same position for the same task type appear in the performance analytics view and are surfaced to department heads. The CANNOT_EXECUTE reason is a required, recorded field — patterns of vague or unsubstantiated reasons are visible.

---

### Risk 3: Subtask proliferation obscuring accountability

**Risk:** A primary executor creates a large tree of subtasks and delegates all work to subordinates. When the parent task is overdue, accountability is diffuse — "I'm waiting for my subtasks" becomes a shield.

**Mitigation:** The three-level depth limit is enforced. The primary responsible executor cannot complete the parent task until all subtasks are in terminal states — the burden is on them to ensure their subtask tree completes, not just to monitor it. Subtask overdue status propagates upward as a flag on the parent task. The responsible executor's performance metric includes the completion rate of their self-created subtask trees.

---

### Risk 4: Cross-department tasks creating invisible workload conflicts

**Risk:** A senior official assigns tasks to positions across multiple departments without awareness of each department's current workload. Departments become overloaded with high-priority cross-departmental obligations while their internal work suffers.

**Mitigation:** Department heads have visibility into all tasks assigned to positions in their department, including cross-department assignments. A workload summary view per department shows total active task count, priority distribution, and upcoming deadline density. This is an analytical visibility feature, not an enforcement mechanism — the authority to assign tasks cross-departmentally is real. The tool makes the workload consequences visible so that resource conflicts can be raised through normal management channels.

---

### Risk 5: EDMS-Task synchronization divergence

**Risk:** An EDMS assignment and its corresponding Task Management task diverge in state due to a failed synchronization event. The EDMS shows the assignment as pending while Task Management shows the task as completed.

**Mitigation:** Synchronization events use the outbox pattern — they are only published after the originating transaction commits, and they are delivered with at-least-once guarantees. Both domains are designed to be idempotent with respect to synchronization events. A reconciliation process runs periodically, comparing EDMS assignment states with corresponding Task Management task states and flagging divergences for `document_admin` review. Divergences are surfaced, not silently resolved, because the appropriate resolution depends on which domain's state is authoritative for the specific divergence.

---

### Trade-off: Position-based assignment vs. user-based assignment

Position-based assignment is organizationally correct and personnel-change-resilient, but it introduces friction when a specific individual is genuinely the right person regardless of position (due to unique expertise, ongoing context, or relationship). The "person-specific flag" on assignments is the concession to this reality. The trade-off is that position-based defaults are correct for most cases, and person-specific exceptions are explicitly modeled rather than being the default assumption.

---

### Trade-off: Strict lifecycle enforcement vs. operational flexibility

A strict state machine prevents invalid state combinations and ensures the history is coherent — but government operations sometimes require rapid responses that don't follow the standard workflow. The ON_HOLD and CANNOT_EXECUTE states, the administrative override capability (for `task_admin` and supervisors), and the configurable Task Type behaviors provide the needed flexibility within the governance model. Complete flexibility (free-form status) would undermine the accountability model entirely.

---

## 19. Final Task Management Architecture

The CoESCD Task Management system is a **position-driven, authority-validated, lifecycle-governed execution control domain** built on five architectural commitments that are non-negotiable for a government-grade accountability system:

---

**Commitment 1 — Authority is validated at every assignment.**
No task may be assigned to a position outside the assigning user's command chain authority without explicit, audited, time-bounded authorization. This rule is enforced by the Authorization domain at assignment time. It is not advisory. It is not a UI warning that can be dismissed. A system that allows authority violations with warnings produces a culture where warnings are ignored and authority boundaries are treated as suggestions.

---

**Commitment 2 — Accountability is singular and immovable.**
Every task has exactly one primary responsible position. Co-executors contribute; the responsible position is accountable. Accountability cannot be shared, distributed, or diluted through subtask creation. The responsible position remains accountable even when they have delegated the operational work to their subordinates. This principle makes escalation logic deterministic, performance analytics meaningful, and supervisory oversight tractable.

---

**Commitment 3 — Every action is a permanent record.**
Assignment, acceptance, progress updates, status changes, deadline modifications, subtask creation, reassignment, escalation, completion, and verification — all of these are events that produce permanent Task History entries. Status fields are not overwritten; they are advanced through state transitions that produce records. The task history tells the complete story of every obligation from inception to closure.

---

**Commitment 4 — Integration is bidirectional and synchronized.**
The Task Management domain does not operate as an island. Document-generated tasks maintain live links to their originating EDMS records. Lifecycle events in Task Management synchronize to EDMS. Completions, delays, and failures in Task Management are immediately surfaced to the supervisory and documentary record. A task that is completed in isolation from its originating document record is an incomplete institutional record.

---

**Commitment 5 — The system surfaces reality, not comfort.**
Overdue tasks are flagged. Escalations reach the right supervisory levels. CANNOT_EXECUTE states are tracked and surfaced analytically. Cross-department workload conflicts are visible to department heads. The system does not smooth over operational failures — it makes them visible precisely so that organizational decision-makers can intervene. A task management system that protects users from seeing bad news is not an accountability system — it is a liability.
